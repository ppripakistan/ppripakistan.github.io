28th Amendment in Pakistan — Have New Provinces Been Approved?

What is verifiable on the public parliamentary record regarding Pakistan’s proposed 28th Amendment, where parties and provincial assemblies stand, and the constitutional sequence Article 239 requires before provincial limits can change.

By PPRI Research Faculty Status checked: 26 November 2026, Pakistan time Citation: PPRI-ART-2026-026 ~7 min read

Current Parliamentary & Constitutional Status

Pakistan’s debate over new provinces has reached the stage of public proposals and political consultations. As of 26 November 2026, this review could not verify a published amendment bill creating new provinces, an approved map, or an official referendum schedule. Article 1 of the Constitution continues to identify Punjab, Sindh, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, and Balochistan as Pakistan’s four provinces. The next development to look for is a public legislative document that identifies the proposed constitutional changes and the territory affected.

Published Amendment Bill
Unverified / not tabled in NA or Senate government bills registers.
Approved Boundary Map
None verified. Article 1’s four provinces remain unchanged.
Official Referendum Schedule
None announced; Article 48(6) joint-sitting referral not enacted.

Sources checked: National Assembly government bills register · Senate government bills register · Constitution of Pakistan (PDF) · Reporting on the proposal’s status (The News)

What is being proposed?

The debate brings together three different ideas. One is to create more provinces, with some ministers discussing configurations of around 12 or 12–15 units. Another is to seek public opinion through a referendum. A third is to strengthen local government and other administrative units within existing provinces. A speaker can support one idea and have reservations about another. A headline that combines all three can give the impression that a single agreed plan exists.

Sources: November reporting on the proposals (Al Jazeera) · Reporting on the district-government alternative (Geo News)

Can a referendum create a province?

Article 48(6) provides a route for a referendum on a matter of national importance. The prime minister may refer the matter to a joint sitting of Parliament. If it is approved there, the question can be put to voters in yes-or-no form. A change to provincial limits still requires the amendment process in Article 239. A national vote can be part of the political discussion. The constitutional votes on an amendment must still take place.

Constitutional text: Constitution of Pakistan, Articles 48 and 239 · Read Article 48 · Read Article 239

What would Article 239 require?

  1. A constitutional amendment bill may begin in either the National Assembly or Senate.
  2. Both Houses must pass the bill with at least two-thirds of their total membership. If the second House amends it, the originating House must pass the amended version by the required majority.
  3. If the bill alters the limits of a province, the affected provincial assembly must also pass it with at least two-thirds of its total membership before it can go to the president.
  4. Once the applicable votes are complete, the bill goes for presidential assent. The enacted text and its commencement provisions establish what changes and when. Constitution, Article 239

What should readers watch for next?

Look for a bill number and text, then for the map or district schedule, parliamentary votes, any affected assembly vote, and the enacted text. Then read the transition provisions. They should explain how representation, budgets, staff, records, assets, liabilities, and services would be handled. A map can show where a province might start and end. Those provisions tell residents what would actually change.
