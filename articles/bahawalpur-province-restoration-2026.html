Bahawalpur Province Restoration 2026: Why the Demand Is Back

A Bahawalpur restoration campaign returned to national headlines in autumn 2026. Its supporters frame the demand through historic identity, 1947 statehood treaties, and regional representation—but political advocacy is not the same as restored constitutional status.

Prepared: October 2026
Reading time: 8 minutes
Research desk: PPRI Regional Demographics Unit

1947 Year of accession to Pakistan as an autonomous princely state

2026 Launch of Bahawalpur Suba Mahaz and renewed restoration rallies

The demand to restore Bahawalpur province returned to public debate alongside wider 2026 calls for administrative restructuring in Pakistan. The movement is distinct from the broader South Punjab proposal because its central argument draws on Bahawalpur’s historic political identity and the legal claim that an autonomous provincial status should be restored rather than created from scratch.

Why Is the Demand Back in 2026?

On 24 August 2026, Dawn reported a major public rally in Bahawalpur demanding the restoration of Bahawalpur as a separate province. Within two weeks, on 7 September, Nawabzada Prince Bahawal Abbasi formally announced the launch of the Bahawalpur Suba Mahaz (Bahawalpur Province Front) campaign, calling on major political parties to honor past parliamentary commitments.

These rallies coincided with federal discussions initiated by the Planning Ministry concerning administrative decentralisation. While the campaign has regained significant regional visibility, public advocacy must be distinguished from completed constitutional amendments: a mass rally demonstrates regional sentiment, but does not alter provincial boundaries without legislative action.

From Princely State to Modern Province Debate: The Chronology

Understanding why the demand has survived for more than half a century requires examining the chronological transformation of Bahawalpur’s constitutional status:

1947: Accession to Pakistan

Nawab Sir Sadiq Muhammad Khan Abbasi V signed the Instrument of Accession in October 1947, preserving internal autonomy while federating defence, external affairs, and communications with Pakistan.

1951: Autonomous Provincial Administration

Under the Government of Bahawalpur Act, the state functioned with an elected 49-member Legislative Assembly and Chief Minister, operating effectively as an autonomous province.

1955: Integration into One Unit

The central government consolidated West Pakistan into a single province ("One Unit"). Bahawalpur surrendered its separate assembly and became an administrative division within West Pakistan.

1970: Dissolution of One Unit & Punjab Merger

When President Yahya Khan dissolved One Unit via the Legal Framework Order, the historic provinces (Sindh, NWFP, Balochistan, Punjab) were reconstituted. However, Bahawalpur was merged into Punjab, triggering widespread public unrest and the formation of the Bahawalpur United Front.

May 2012: Punjab Assembly Dual Resolutions

The Punjab Provincial Assembly passed historic cross-party resolutions formally supporting both the creation of a South Punjab province and the separate restoration of Bahawalpur province.

August–September 2026: Suba Mahaz Mobilisation

Renewed public rallies in Bahawalpur, Ahmedpur East, and Rahim Yar Khan organized under the Bahawalpur Suba Mahaz asserting historical treaty rights.

Bahawalpur Province vs South Punjab: Comparing the Two Models

The primary friction in the southern Punjab debate is between those who envision a unified 11-district province and those who insist on a separate 3-district Bahawalpur entity:

Institutional Feature Standalone Bahawalpur Province Unified South Punjab Province
Territorial Scope 3 Districts: Bahawalpur, Bahawalnagar, Rahim Yar Khan. 11 Districts: Multan, D.G. Khan, and Bahawalpur divisions.
Census 2023 Population 13.40 million citizens (~10.5% of Punjab). 40.38 million citizens (~31.6% of Punjab).
Land Area 45,588 km² (dominated by the Cholistan Desert and Sutlej basin). ~99,500 km² (~48% of Punjab's entire territory).
Capital City Bahawalpur City (historic administrative and palace seat). Multan (or contentious dual-capital split with Bahawalpur).
Regional Identity Bahawalpuri / Riyasati historical identity. Unified administrative / Saraiki identity.

Institutional & Fiscal Viability Scorecard

A credible restoration proposal must address the economic and institutional practicalities of establishing a standalone federating unit:

1. Recurrent Assembly Overhead

Establishing an independent provincial assembly, Governor House, High Court bench, and civil secretariat will absorb significant capital expenditure before delivering service improvements.

2. Divisible Pool Entitlement

With 13.4M citizens, an independent NFC share based 82% on population would guarantee substantial transfers, but own-source tax collection (currently <7% of local spend) requires major reform.

3. Canal Water Rights

Water distribution from the Panjnad barrage and Sutlej canal networks would require separate representation under the 1991 Indus River System Authority (IRSA) Accord.

4. Article 239(4) Legislative Hurdle

Altering Punjab’s limits to excise Bahawalpur requires a two-thirds supermajority in the Punjab Assembly—meaning northern and central Punjab MPAs must vote affirmatively.

Frequently Asked Questions

Was Bahawalpur historically a province?

Yes. Following its 1947 accession, Bahawalpur functioned with an elected provincial assembly and Chief Minister until the 1955 One Unit merger.

Why was it merged into Punjab in 1970?

When One Unit was dissolved, the military administration restored the four main provinces but integrated Bahawalpur into Punjab as an administrative division.

What is Bahawalpur Suba Mahaz?

A regional political front revived in 2026 to campaign for the constitutional restoration of Bahawalpur’s independent provincial boundaries.

Does the Punjab Assembly support restoration?

In May 2012, the Punjab Assembly passed a resolution supporting Bahawalpur's restoration, though constitutional amendment bills were never enacted.
