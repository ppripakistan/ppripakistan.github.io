Is Gilgit-Baltistan a Province of Pakistan? GB, AJK and Islamabad Explained

Gilgit-Baltistan has regional government institutions, but it is not one of Pakistan’s four constitutional provinces. The same basic counting question requires a separate constitutional answer for Azad Jammu and Kashmir and Islamabad Capital Territory.

The direct constitutional answer to “Is Gilgit-Baltistan a province of Pakistan?” is no. Gilgit-Baltistan is not one of the four federating provinces defined in Article 1 of Pakistan’s 1973 Constitution. It possesses a distinct administrative and legal framework, which is fundamentally different from holding the status of a fifth province.

Constitutional Status Hierarchy: Four Distinct Legal Entities

In public discourse, people often casually refer to “the five provinces” or group GB and AJK together. Constitutional jurisprudence distinguishes four clear categories:

1. The Four Constitutional Provinces

Under Article 1(2)(a), the territories of Pakistan include the Provinces of Balochistan, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, Punjab, and Sindh. They hold constitutionally protected NFC divisible pool entitlements and Senate equal representation.

2. Islamabad Capital Territory

Under Article 1(2)(b), ICT is designated as the seat of the federal government. It is not a fifth province, but its citizens participate directly in electing the federal National Assembly and Senate.

3. Gilgit-Baltistan (Special Status)

Administratively empowered through executive presidential orders (2009, 2018). While proposals for "provisional provincial status" have been debated, its constitutional linkage remains formally tied to the UN resolutions on the Jammu & Kashmir dispute.

4. Azad Jammu & Kashmir

A self-governing entity under the 1974 Interim Constitution Act with its own President, Prime Minister, official flag, and Supreme Court. It is not federated as a province of Pakistan.

What Is the "Provisional Provincial Status" Debate?

Because Gilgit-Baltistan’s population (~1.5 million) lacks voting representation in Pakistan’s federal parliament, regional political parties and youth movements have long demanded full constitutional rights:

  • The 2020–2021 High-Powered Committee: A federal committee headed by the Ministry of Kashmir Affairs and Gilgit-Baltistan proposed drafting a constitutional amendment to grant GB provisional provincial status, giving it representation in the National Assembly and Senate without prejudicing Pakistan's stance at the United Nations.
  • Why It Remains Unimplemented: Amending Article 1 to integrate GB into the federation requires navigating sensitive international legal obligations under United Nations Security Council (UNSC) resolutions, as well as securing cross-party two-thirds supermajorities in Parliament.

Why Precision Matters in the New-Provinces Debate

When policymakers discuss creating new administrative units (such as dividing Punjab into South Punjab or Hazara), they are debating internal administrative reorganization governed by Article 239(4). Conversely, granting full provincial status to Gilgit-Baltistan is an external constitutional question intertwined with international territorial treaties. Confusing the two harms both policy clarity and constitutional precision.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Gilgit-Baltistan the 5th province of Pakistan?

No. Gilgit-Baltistan has not been added to Article 1 of the Constitution and remains governed under special administrative orders.

Does Gilgit-Baltistan have members in Pakistan’s Senate?

No. Because it is not a constitutional province, GB has no voting seats in the National Assembly or the Senate of Pakistan.

Is Azad Kashmir a province of Pakistan?

No. AJK operates as an autonomous self-governing state under its own 1974 Interim Constitution Act.

Where should official legal status be verified?

Consult the official text of the 1973 Constitution on Pakistan Code, the Government of Pakistan portal, and official Gazette notifications.

Legal Verification Note: This explainer relies on the current consolidated text of the Constitution of the Islamic Republic of Pakistan, the Government of Gilgit-Baltistan Order 2018, and the Azad Jammu and Kashmir Interim Constitution Act 1974.
