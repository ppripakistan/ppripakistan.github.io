Pakistan’s literacy rate — why the Census says 60.65% and HIES says 63%

Both figures measure Pakistan’s population aged 10 years and older, yet researchers and students regularly mix them. Here is what each Pakistan Bureau of Statistics (PBS) release measures and how to cite each accurately.

1. Why do official literacy figures differ?

Two official Pakistan Bureau of Statistics (PBS) publications report two different national literacy numbers for Pakistan’s population aged 10 years and older: the 2023 Population and Housing Census (Table 4.20) reports 60.65%, while the Household Integrated Economic Survey (HIES 2024–25 Social Indicators Report, Section 2.4.1) reports 63%.

They differ because they come from different statistical instruments rather than contradicting each other:

  • Complete enumeration vs. sample survey: The 2023 Census attempted a full population count across every administrative unit and published exact two-decimal national and subnational figures (National Census Report 2023, Table 4.20). HIES 2024–25 interviewed a representative national household sample designed to estimate living standards quickly between censuses (HIES 2024–25 Social Indicators Report).
  • Coverage timing and questionnaire context: Census enumeration took place in 2023, whereas HIES field collection covers the 2024–25 survey year using detailed household education modules.
  • Precision convention: The HIES Social Indicators Report publishes summary percentages rounded to whole numbers (63% national; 68% Punjab; 58% Sindh; 58% KP; 49% Balochistan), whereas the Census reports exact two-decimal figures (60.65%).

2. Which literacy figure should I use?

Selecting the right series depends on the analytical question you are answering. Never blend district census stock figures with national survey percentages in the same calculation row.

Decision guide: selecting and citing the right official literacy figure
If your question is… Use… How to write it
What is Pakistan’s complete census-count literacy baseline or district benchmark? Census 2023 (60.65%) “Pakistan’s 2023 National Census (Table 4.20) recorded a national literacy rate of 60.65% among the population aged 10 and above.”
What is the most recent post-census survey estimate and trend since 2018–19? PBS HIES 2024–25 (63%) “PBS’s 2024–25 HIES Social Indicators Report (§2.4.1) estimates national literacy (age 10+) at 63%, up from 60% in HIES 2018–19.”
How large are urban–rural and male–female disparities today? PBS HIES 2024–25 breakdowns “HIES 2024–25 reports a 19 percentage-point urban–rural gap (74% urban vs. 55% rural) and a 19 percentage-point gender gap (73% male vs. 54% female).”

3. What the HIES 2024–25 figures show

Even where national headlines celebrate a modest survey rise from 60% in 2018–19 to 63% in 2024–25, structural disparities across provinces, urban–rural settlements, and gender remain stark. Both the urban–rural gap (74% vs. 55%) and the male–female gap (73% vs. 54%) stand at 19 percentage points.

4. Literacy, adult literacy, and school attendance

A frequent pitfall in policy briefs and exam answers is treating Pakistan’s standard 10+ literacy rate as interchangeable with adult literacy (15+) or school enrolment ratios:

  • Pakistan standard (age 10+): Both the 2023 Census (60.65%) and HIES 2024–25 (63%) measure whether persons aged 10 years and older can read and write a short simple statement with understanding and perform basic arithmetic.
  • International adult literacy (age 15+): Global SDG tables often report age 15+, which excludes children aged 10–14 and therefore produces a slightly lower national percentage than age 10+ headline figures.
  • School attendance vs. stock literacy: Net Enrolment Rate (NER) or Gross Enrolment Rate (GER) captures children currently attending school in an academic year, whereas literacy reflects cumulative educational attainment across every living cohort aged 10 and older—which is why adult illiteracy changes far more slowly than current primary enrolment. For an exam application connecting Article 25A with HIES social indicators, see PPRI’s CSS Pakistan Affairs 18th Amendment Study Guide.

5. A useful citation for CSS and journalism

Recommended model wording (copy-ready):

“While Pakistan’s 2023 National Census recorded a baseline literacy rate of 60.65% (population aged 10+, Table 4.20), the subsequent sample-based PBS HIES 2024–25 (§2.4.1) places national literacy at 63%—up from 60% in HIES 2018–19—with persistent 19 percentage-point gaps between urban (74%) and rural (55%) residents and between men (73%) and women (54%).”

اردو خلاصہ — مردم شماری (60.65 فیصد) بمقابلہ ایچ آئی ای ایس سروے (63 فیصد)

پاکستان بیورو آف اسٹیٹسٹکس (PBS) کے دو سرکاری اعداد و شمار بظاہر مختلف نظر آتے ہیں مگر دونوں 10 سال یا اس سے زیادہ عمر کی آبادی میں خواندگی کی پیمائش کرتے ہیں۔ مردم شماری 2023 (ٹیبل 4.20) مکمل گنتی پر مبنی ہے جس میں خواندگی کی شرح 60.65 فیصد درج ہوئی، جبکہ HIES سروے 2024–25 (§2.4.1) گھرانوں کے نمونہ جاتی سروے پر مبنی ہے جس کے مطابق قومی شرح خواندگی 63 فیصد ہے۔ ان دونوں مختلف ذرائع کے درمیان براہِ راست فرق نکال کر اسے سالانہ ترقی قرار نہیں دینا چاہیے؛ سروے کے اندر رجحان جانچنے کے لیے HIES 2018–19 (60 فیصد) کا موازنہ HIES 2024–25 (63 فیصد) سے کیا جاتا ہے۔ تازہ سروے کے مطابق شہری (74 فیصد) اور دیہی (55 فیصد) علاقوں کے درمیان، اور مردوں (73 فیصد) اور خواتین (54 فیصد) کے درمیان 19 فیصد پوائنٹس کا نمایاں فرق برقرار ہے۔ تفصیلی صوبائی جدول اور ڈاؤن لوڈ ایبل ڈیٹا کے لیے پاکستان کی شرحِ خواندگی کی اردو گائیڈ دیکھیں۔
