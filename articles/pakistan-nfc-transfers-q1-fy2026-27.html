How much did each province receive under the NFC? Q1 FY2026–27 figures

Provisional Finance Division fiscal accounts for July–September 2026 show Rs 1.895 trillion in combined NFC transfers—alongside a striking divergence between Punjab, Sindh, and KP surpluses and Balochistan’s first-quarter deficit.

1. Provincial transfers and fiscal balances

In the first quarter of FY2026–27 (July–September 2026, provisional), federal transfers under the National Finance Commission (NFC) award totaled Rs 1,895,236 million (Rs 1,895.24 billion), according to the Ministry of Finance’s Summary of Consolidated and Provincial Fiscal Operations (Tables 5–9, PDF). Because NFC disbursements remain the backbone of subnational budgets, quarterly transfers are frequently mistaken for fiscal surpluses—yet overall balances depend equally on provincial own revenue and total quarterly spending.

2. How can provinces receive transfers and still report a deficit?

An NFC transfer is a federal revenue inflow—not a fiscal surplus by itself. Each province’s overall fiscal balance compares its total revenue (NFC transfers plus provincial tax and non-tax collections) against its total quarterly expenditure (current operating spending such as salaries, subsidies, and pensions plus development outlays).

Across all four provinces combined in Q1 FY2026–27, provinces received Rs 1,895.24 billion in federal NFC transfers alongside Rs 501.59 billion in other reported provincial revenue, producing Rs 2,396.83 billion in total receipts against Rs 1,797.01 billion in total spending—yielding a combined surplus of +Rs 599.82 billion. Balochistan recorded a negative balance (−Rs 16.20 billion) simply because its first-quarter expenditure outlays exceeded its combined Q1 federal transfers and provincial receipts.

3. Why the award weights and quarterly figures look different

Readers often notice that a province’s share of quarterly NFC releases in Table 5 does not match the statutory four-factor horizontal weights in the 7th NFC Award (51.74% Punjab, 24.55% Sindh, 14.62% KP, 9.09% Balochistan) down to two decimals. Several legal and accounting mechanisms explain the difference:

  • Undivided 1% War-on-Terror Allocation: Before calculating horizontal shares out of the divisible pool, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa receives 1% of the net divisible pool for security/war-on-terror costs—lifting KP’s effective quarterly share above its 14.62% horizontal formula slice (to 16.58% of four-province NFC transfers in Q1 FY2026–27).
  • Straight Transfers & Special Grants: Divisible pool transfers appear alongside straight transfers (natural gas development surcharge, oil/gas royalties, and excise duty) and subventions, whose collection and release schedules fluctuate quarter by quarter.
  • Provisional Q1 Timing: Quarterly provincial cash releases are provisional settlement accounts adjusted against actual FBR tax collections over subsequent quarters of the fiscal year.

4. What do these numbers tell residents?

Quarterly cash surpluses at the consolidated provincial level do not automatically mean local classrooms, clinics, or water schemes are fully funded. Much of Pakistan’s quarterly provincial surplus reflects cash held to help meet combined consolidated IMF/National Fiscal Pact budget targets, delayed development project disbursements early in the fiscal year, and deferred municipal transfers under Provincial Finance Commission (PFC) formulas.
