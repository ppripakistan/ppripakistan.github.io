Provinces of Pakistan 2026: Names, Capitals, Population and Area

As of 2026, Pakistan has 4 constitutional provinces (Punjab, Sindh, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, and Balochistan), 1 federal capital territory (Islamabad), and 2 autonomous administrative territories (Azad Jammu & Kashmir and Gilgit-Baltistan), serving a total population of 241.49 million (PBS Census 2023). This reference guide provides their capitals, Census 2023 populations, and territorial areas, and explains why Islamabad, Gilgit-Baltistan, and Azad Jammu and Kashmir cannot simply be counted as additional provinces.

Which is the Largest Province in Pakistan?

The answer depends on whether size is measured by land area or population:

  • Largest by Land Area: Balochistan is by far the largest province, spanning 347,190 square kilometres (nearly 44% of Pakistan’s total land area). However, with 14.89 million people, it is the least populous province, creating vast administrative distances between towns and service centres.
  • Largest by Population: Punjab is the largest province by population, holding 127.69 million people—representing more than 52% of Pakistan’s total inhabitants. Governed from Lahore, its demographic mass exceeds the sovereign populations of the United Kingdom, France, or Germany.

Constitutional Classification: Understanding the Territorial Hierarchy

In common conversation, people sometimes ask whether Islamabad, Gilgit-Baltistan, or Azad Kashmir are “the 5th or 6th provinces.” Legally, each occupies a distinct constitutional category:

1. Four Constitutional Provinces

Punjab, Sindh, KP, Balochistan: Full federating units under Article 1. Represented equally in the Senate (23 seats each) and entitled to the National Finance Commission (NFC) divisible pool.

2. Islamabad Capital Territory (ICT)

Federal Capital: Defined under Article 1(2)(b) as federal territory. It has 3 National Assembly seats and 4 Senate seats, but no provincial assembly or chief minister.

3. Gilgit-Baltistan (GB)

Special Administrative Territory: Governed under presidential governance orders with its own elected assembly, but not currently a constitutional province under Article 1.

4. Azad Jammu & Kashmir (AJK)

Self-Governing State: Operates under its own 1974 Interim Constitution Act with a separate President, Prime Minister, Assembly, and Supreme Court.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many provinces does Pakistan have in 2026?

Pakistan has four constitutional provinces: Punjab, Sindh, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, and Balochistan.

Is Islamabad a province?

No. Islamabad is the federal capital territory administered directly by the federal government.

Is Gilgit-Baltistan the 5th province?

No. While constitutional status proposals have been debated, it remains a distinct administrative territory.

What is Pakistan’s total population?

The official 2023 Census recorded Pakistan’s national population at 241,499,431 (including 2.36M in Islamabad).

Frequently Asked Questions & Governance Insights

Verified answers to common public inquiries regarding administrative scale, constitutional procedure, and demographic benchmarks:

How many provinces are in Pakistan in 2026?

As of 2026, Pakistan officially has 4 constitutional provinces: Punjab, Sindh, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, and Balochistan, established under Article 1 of the Constitution of 1973. In addition to the four provinces, Pakistan includes 1 federal capital territory (Islamabad Capital Territory) and 2 autonomous administrative territories (Azad Jammu & Kashmir and Gilgit-Baltistan).

What are the capitals of Pakistan's provinces?

The provincial capitals of Pakistan are Lahore for Punjab, Karachi for Sindh, Peshawar for Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, and Quetta for Balochistan. The federal national capital of the Islamic Republic of Pakistan is Islamabad.

What is the largest province of Pakistan by population and land area?

By population, Punjab is the largest province with 127,688,922 citizens (52.88% of national population) according to the 7th Census 2023. By territorial land area, Balochistan is the largest province, covering 347,190 square kilometers (43.6% of Pakistan's total land area), but with only 14.89 million residents.

Why are Gilgit-Baltistan and Azad Kashmir not counted as constitutional provinces?

Gilgit-Baltistan and Azad Jammu and Kashmir (AJK) possess distinct constitutional and administrative frameworks linked to the United Nations resolutions on the Jammu and Kashmir dispute. They have their own legislative assemblies and prime ministers/chief ministers, but are not formally enumerated as constitutional provinces under Article 1 of Pakistan's 1973 Constitution.
