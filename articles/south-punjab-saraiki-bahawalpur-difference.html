South Punjab vs Saraiki Province vs Bahawalpur: What’s the Difference?

The terms South Punjab, Saraiki province, and Bahawalpur province frequently appear together in Pakistan’s new-provinces debate—but they do not necessarily describe the same political project or constitutional outcome.

If you are searching for the difference between South Punjab, Saraiki province, and Bahawalpur province, the short answer is that the three demands overlap geographically but differ fundamentally in their core rationale, political framing, and preferred constitutional outcomes.

Core Dimension South Punjab Province Saraiki Province Bahawalpur Province
Primary Rationale Administrative decentralisation, geographic proximity, and regional development parity. Ethno-linguistic recognition, cultural preservation, and linguistic community representation. Restoration of historic pre-1955 constitutional status and accession treaty rights.

Envisaged Capital Multan (economic centre) or dual capital arrangement with Bahawalpur. Multan (historic cultural heart of the Saraiki belt). Bahawalpur City (historic royal capital and administrative seat).
Constitutional Basis New administrative province created via Article 239 amendment to Punjab boundaries. Ethno-linguistic boundary reorganization under federal constitutional reform. Restoration claim citing 1947 Instrument of Accession and post-One Unit commitments.
Current Legal Status Active demand; partial executive compromise via 2020 South Punjab Secretariat. Political and cultural movement; not an officially gazetted boundary bill. Active restoration campaign (Bahawalpur Suba Mahaz); currently part of Punjab.

1. The South Punjab Demand: Administrative Devolution

The South Punjab proposal is primarily framed around governance overload and administrative distance. Proponents point out that Punjab holds 127.69 million people—over 52% of Pakistan’s population—governed from a single secretariat in Lahore:

  • Human Development Gaps: As documented in PPRI’s South Punjab Deep Dive, multidimensional poverty in southern tehsils reaches up to 2.5 times the central Punjab average.
  • Institutional Compromise: In 2020, the Punjab government established the South Punjab Secretariat with an Additional Chief Secretary (ACS) and Additional IG operating across Multan and Bahawalpur, demonstrating administrative demand while stopping short of territorial division.

2. The Saraiki Province Demand: Identity and Language

The Saraiki province movement approaches the question through cultural and linguistic rights. It emphasizes that the Saraiki-speaking population forms a distinct linguistic community with centuries of shared literary, musical, and social heritage:

  • Cultural Recognition: Proponents argue that Pakistan’s other four provinces broadly align with major ethno-linguistic identities (Punjabi, Sindhi, Pashtun, Balochi), making a Saraiki-majority federating unit a logical constitutional recognition.
  • Territorial Ambiguity: Unlike the neat administrative boundary of the three southern divisions, linguistic boundaries are fluid. Proponents sometimes include Mianwali and Bhakkar from Sargodha division, or Dera Ismail Khan from Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, creating interprovincial complexities.

3. The Bahawalpur Demand: Historic Restoration

The Bahawalpur restoration movement differs fundamentally from the other two because it rejects being classified as a "new" province. Instead, it demands the restoration of an autonomous status that historically existed:

  • Princely State Accession (1947): The State of Bahawalpur was the first princely state to accede to Pakistan in October 1947, operating as an autonomous entity until the One Unit scheme consolidated West Pakistan in 1955.
  • The 1970 Dissolution Grievance: When General Yahya Khan dissolved One Unit in 1970, the former provinces of Sindh, NWFP, Balochistan, and Punjab were restored, but Bahawalpur was merged into Punjab as an administrative division, sparking immediate protest movements that persist to this day.

The Core Dilemma: One Province or Two?

Any legislative bill seeking to partition southern Punjab immediately faces an internal geographic rivalry: Proponents of a unified 11-district South Punjab want Multan as the principal capital, while Bahawalpur restorationists refuse to be governed from Multan instead of Lahore. Any durable constitutional solution must openly reconcile this Multan–Bahawalpur capital question.

PPRI 3-Pillar Evaluation Scorecard

To help policymakers and citizens evaluate these competing proposals objectively, PPRI structures the debate across three institutional pillars:

Pillar 1: Governance Proximity

Will establishing a new provincial capital actually improve primary healthcare, canal water access, and school enrollment in rural union councils, or merely concentrate power in Multan?

Pillar 2: Fiscal & NFC Realities

How will the divisible pool under Article 160 (NFC) be partitioned? Can a standalone Bahawalpur province (13.4M people) generate sufficient own-source revenue to finance a full assembly and secretariat?

Pillar 3: Article 239(4) Supermajority

Does the proposal possess cross-party political support capable of securing two-thirds supermajorities in both houses of Parliament and the Punjab Provincial Assembly?

Frequently Asked Questions

Are South Punjab and Saraiki province identical?

No. South Punjab is an administrative proposal based on 3 divisions, while Saraiki province is rooted in ethno-linguistic identity and may include different boundaries.

Why does Bahawalpur want a separate province?

Bahawalpur advocates claim historic restoration of the pre-1955 autonomous statehood that was integrated into Punjab upon One Unit dissolution in 1970.

What is the South Punjab Secretariat?

An executive arrangement established in 2020 featuring an Additional Chief Secretary and Additional IG operating across Multan and Bahawalpur without creating a new assembly.

Has any boundary bill been passed in 2026?

No. While committee discussions occurred in the Senate, no constitutional amendment has been voted upon or enacted.

Frequently Asked Questions & Governance Insights

Verified policy answers regarding constitutional principles, empirical evidence, and administrative implications.

What is the difference between the South Punjab province and Saraiki province concepts?

The South Punjab concept is primarily administrative and geographic, encompassing Multan, D.G. Khan, and Bahawalpur divisions. The Saraiki province movement emphasizes linguistic and cultural identity and has historically included Mianwali and Bhakkar.

Why does Bahawalpur resist being merged into a single South Punjab province?

Bahawalpur advocates emphasize its historical status as an autonomous princely state until 1955 and express concern that a unified South Punjab province governed from Multan would repeat the marginalization experienced under Lahore.

What consensus model reconciles Multan, D.G. Khan, and Bahawalpur aspirations?

A dual-administrative model or elevating existing divisions into autonomous sub-units with independent municipal fiscal powers provides the highest level of regional consensus.
