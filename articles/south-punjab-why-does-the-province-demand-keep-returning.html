South Punjab: Why Does the Province Demand Keep Returning?

South Punjab’s province demand has survived changes of government, political parties, and election cycles. Its persistence is easier to understand when regional inequality, administrative distance, identity, and constitutional history are evaluated together.

By PPRI Research Faculty Published: October 2026 Citation: PPRI-ART-2026-007 ~11 min read
40.4 M Combined population of Multan, Bahawalpur, and D.G. Khan divisions (Census 2023)
~45% Geographic share of Punjab’s land area concentrated in the southern region
2.5× Poverty incidence in South Punjab relative to the provincial average (UNDP)
44% Child stunting prevalence across southern districts documented by regional surveys

South Punjab is the clearest and most durable example of why Pakistan’s provincial restructuring debate refuses to disappear.

The demand is neither a sudden political novelty nor a transient electoral gimmick. In May 2012, the National Assembly passed Resolution 62 calling for the establishment of a Janoobi Punjab province to address deep-seated regional grievances. Within days, the Punjab Assembly passed corresponding resolutions supporting both a South Punjab province and the restoration of Bahawalpur’s historic provincial status.

Although that parliamentary momentum stalled without a constitutional amendment, the demand never evaporated. It persisted across subsequent general elections, party manifestos, the establishment of the South Punjab Secretariat in 2020, and the renewed 2026 federal restructuring dialogue. Understanding its endurance requires looking beyond political rhetoric into demographic scale, spatial geography, and human development indicators.

South Punjab Population & District Breakdown (Census 2023)

Under official PBS Census 2023 data, the proposed South Punjab administrative unit encompasses three administrative divisions and 11 to 13 districts, representing over 40.4 million residents (31.7% of Punjab's total population):

Division Headquarters Districts Included Population (2023 Census) Share of Punjab (%)
Multan Division Multan Multan, Khanewal, Lodhran, Vehari 13,568,784 10.63%
Bahawalpur Division Bahawalpur Bahawalpur, Bahawalnagar, Rahim Yar Khan 12,746,242 9.98%
Dera Ghazi Khan Division D.G. Khan D.G. Khan, Layyah, Muzaffargarh, Kot Addu, Taunsa 14,112,658 11.05%
South Punjab (3-Division Total) Multan / Bahawalpur 11 Core Districts (12 with Kot Addu / Taunsa) 40,427,684 31.66%

With 40.4 million residents, a standalone South Punjab province would rank as Pakistan's third-largest province—substantially larger than Khyber Pakhtunkhwa (40.86M) and nearly triple the population of Balochistan (14.89M).

The demographic scale of South Punjab

According to the official 7th Population and Housing Census 2023, the three administrative divisions comprising the southern belt hold staggering populations:

  • Multan Division: 14.09 million citizens (Districts: Multan, Khanewal, Lodhran, Vehari)
  • Bahawalpur Division: 13.40 million citizens (Districts: Bahawalpur, Bahawalnagar, Rahim Yar Khan)
  • Dera Ghazi Khan Division: 12.89 million citizens (Districts: D.G. Khan, Layyah, Muzaffargarh, Rajanpur, Kot Addu, Taunsa)

Together, these three divisions encompass 40.38 million people—representing nearly 32% of Punjab’s total population of 127.69 million, and covering approximately 45% of its entire territorial expanse.

To contextualize this scale: a prospective South Punjab province would be substantially more populous than Khyber Pakhtunkhwa (40.85M with merged districts), and almost four times the size of Balochistan (14.89M). On a global scale, it would dwarf sovereign nations like Canada, Poland, and Malaysia. This is not a marginal regional lobby seeking symbolic representation; it is a massive demographic unit governed from a provincial secretariat situated hundreds of kilometres away in Lahore.

The documented human development gap

The core driver of South Punjab’s political alienation is not abstract geography, but acute human development disparity. In its South Punjab Regional Development Plan, the United Nations Development Programme (UNDP) presented an evidence base documenting systematic divergence between southern districts and central/northern Punjab:

Constitutional and legislative history (1970–2026)

The quest for provincial restructuring in South Punjab has a rich legislative timeline across federal and provincial assemblies:

1970: Dissolution of One-Unit & Bahawalpur Merger

Upon the dissolution of the One-Unit scheme, the former princely State of Bahawalpur was integrated into Punjab rather than restored as an independent administrative province, triggering immediate regional protest movements.

May 2012: National Assembly Resolution 62

Federal parliament unanimously adopted a cross-party resolution urging the creation of a new province of Janoobi Punjab to satisfy regional economic and administrative aspirations.

May 2012: Punjab Assembly Dual Resolutions

Punjab Assembly endorsed the South Punjab demand while also formally resolving to restore Bahawalpur’s historic provincial status, establishing the dual-province debate.

June 2020: Establishment of South Punjab Secretariat

An administrative compromise established an Additional Chief Secretary (ACS) and Additional Inspector General of Police (AIG) operating across dual offices in Multan and Bahawalpur.

2025–2026: Senate Constitutional Bill & Renewed Restructuring

A constitutional bill was introduced in the Senate proposing formal provincial boundaries, coinciding with federal dialogues on competitive federalism and subnational scale.

Auditing the Secretariat experiment

The 2020 establishment of the South Punjab Secretariat was an explicit test of administrative decentralisation without territorial division:

Institutional Dimension Policy Intention Observed Practical Reality Assessment
Citizen Proximity Eliminate the need for citizens to travel 400+ km to Lahore for routine approvals. Offices functional in Multan and Bahawalpur; basic grievance redressal improved. Positive progress on administrative access.
Financial Sanctioning Authorize ACS South Punjab to sanction major capital development schemes locally. Major project approvals (over prescribed ceilings) still require provincial cabinet and P&D Lahore clearance. Partial fiscal delegation; key purse strings retained in Lahore.
Departmental Scope Full mirror secretariat spanning all 40+ provincial portfolios. Only 16 key departments established; remaining portfolios still managed exclusively from Lahore. Fragmented administrative authority.
Dual Capital Friction Equitable institutional sharing between Multan and Bahawalpur. Bureaucrats splitting working weeks between two headquarters 100 km apart, causing procedural friction. Operational inefficiency and logistical drag.

The Bahawalpur vs Multan geographic question

One of the primary institutional complexities of the South Punjab debate is that the term does not imply a single consensus boundary:

Proponents of a unified 11-district Saraiki / South Punjab province envisage Multan as the natural economic hub. Conversely, advocates of the Bahawalpur Restoration movement argue that the three districts of Bahawalpur Division (Bahawalpur, Bahawalnagar, Rahim Yar Khan) possess a distinct constitutional status stemming from the 1947 accession instruments and deserve their own independent provincial entity.

Any serious legislative proposal must openly reconcile this internal territorial question rather than papering over it with broad slogans.

PPRI 8-Point Empirical Evaluation Scorecard

To determine whether establishing a South Punjab province provides a superior solution to empowered local government, PPRI proposes eight empirical benchmarks:

1. Legislative Parity

Would creation of South Punjab entitle the region to equal representation (23 seats) in the Senate of Pakistan, balancing federal policy influence?

2. Dedicated NFC Allocation

Would an independent divisible pool entitlement eliminate the risk of mid-year development fund diversion to northern megaprojects?

3. Recurrent Overhead Burden

Can the region finance the capital establishment of a new assembly, high court seat, and public service commission without eating into health and education?

4. Water & Canal Rights

How will Indus River canal irrigation rights under the 1991 Water Apportionment Accord be shared between Punjab and South Punjab?

5. Internal Devolution

Will the new provincial government empower municipal corporations in Multan, D.G. Khan, and Bahawalpur, or recreate Lahore-style centralism locally?

6. Asset & Debt Division

What is the equitable formula for partitioning Punjab’s sovereign foreign debts, pension liabilities, and physical infrastructure assets?

7. Service Delivery Velocity

Will infant mortality, school enrollment, and clean water access improve faster under a separate provincial cabinet than under empowered local councils?

8. Democratic Consensus

Does the proposal meet the constitutional threshold of Article 239(4)(4)—securing a two-thirds supermajority in the Punjab Assembly?

Frequently Asked Questions & Governance Insights

Verified answers to common public inquiries regarding administrative scale, constitutional procedure, and demographic benchmarks:

What is the total population of South Punjab according to Census 2023?

According to the 2023 Census, the proposed South Punjab region—comprising the three administrative divisions of Multan, Bahawalpur, and Dera Ghazi Khan—has a combined population of 40,427,684 citizens (approximately 31.7% of Punjab's total population).

Which districts are included in the proposed South Punjab province?

The proposed South Punjab province consists of 11 core administrative districts (expanded to 13 with newly formed districts): Multan, Khanewal, Lodhran, Vehari, Bahawalpur, Bahawalnagar, Rahim Yar Khan, Dera Ghazi Khan, Layyah, Muzaffargarh, Kot Addu, and Taunsa.

What is the constitutional procedure to create a South Punjab province?

Under Article 239(4) of the 1973 Constitution, an amendment bill altering the limits of a province must be passed by both the National Assembly and Senate with a two-thirds majority, and must also be approved by a two-thirds majority of the total membership of the provincial assembly of the affected province (the Provincial Assembly of the Punjab).

What is the South Punjab Secretariat established in 2020?

In 2020, the Punjab government established dual South Punjab secretariats in Multan and Bahawalpur, headed by an Additional Chief Secretary (ACS) and Additional Inspector General of Police (Addl. IGP). However, full financial powers and legislative autonomy were never devolved, maintaining primary budget and administrative authority in Lahore.
