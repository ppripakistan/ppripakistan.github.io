Article 140A: Local Government

Read Article 140A in Full Reader →

Flagship devolution mandate inserted by the 18th Amendment: Each Province shall, by law, establish a local government system and devolve political, administrative and financial responsibility and authority to the elected representatives of the local governments. Clause (2) mandates the Election Commission of Pakistan to hold local government elections.

Core Salient Features of the 1973 Constitution

Adopted on 14 August 1973 and restored to authentic federalism by the 18th Amendment, the Constitution provides the framework for democracy, federalism, human rights, and the rule of law.

Governance

Federal Parliamentary Republic

Executive authority is exercised by the Prime Minister and Cabinet collectively responsible to the National Assembly (Arts. 90 & 91). The President acts strictly on advice (Art. 48); arbitrary dissolution power under Article 58(2)(b) is permanently abolished.

Rights

Enforceable Fundamental Rights

Articles 8–28 guarantee core civil liberties enforceable through High Court writ jurisdiction (Art. 199). Enriched by Article 9A (Clean Environment), 10A (Fair Trial), 19A (Right to Information), and 25A (Right to Education).

State Ideology

Islamic Character & Injunctions

Declares Islam as State religion (Article 2) and incorporates the Objectives Resolution as a substantive provision (Article 2A). Mandates conformity of laws with Quran and Sunnah (Art. 227) while protecting minority personal laws.

Federalism

Provincial Autonomy & Fiscal Devolution

Abolished the Concurrent List, devolving residual legislative power to Provinces. Enforces elected local government devolution (Article 140A), the NFC non-retrogression floor (Article 160(3A)), and 50:50 natural resources joint ownership (Article 172(3)).

Judicature

Superior Judicial Architecture

Guarantees judicial separation from the executive (Article 175). Post-26th and 27th Amendments formalize the Federal Constitutional Court alongside the Supreme Court of Pakistan and dedicated Constitutional Benches for constitutional interpretation.

Rigidity

Rigid Amendment Procedure

Requires a two-thirds majority of total membership in both Houses (Article 239). Any amendment altering provincial boundaries strictly requires passage by two-thirds of the total membership of the affected Provincial Assembly (Article 239(4)).

Constitutional Core Pillar

Part II: Fundamental Rights Sanctuary (Articles 8–28)

Read in 280-Article Reader →

The supreme inviolable compact. Article 8 voids any statutory law inconsistent with these rights. Guarantees security of person (Art. 9), clean environment (Art. 9A), safeguards upon arrest (Art. 10), fair trial and due process (Art. 10A), inviolability of dignity and privacy (Art. 14), core freedoms of assembly, speech, association, and information (Arts 15–19A), and equal rights with free compulsory education (Arts 25 & 25A).
