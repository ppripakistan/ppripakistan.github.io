Public Consultation

Frequently Asked Questions

Plain-language explanations of PPRI's public consultation process, citizen submission standards, institutional independence, and research methodology.

Core Commitment

PPRI is an independent, non-partisan research platform. The public consultation process is designed to gather structured citizen questions, evidence, and priorities — without advancing predetermined outcomes or partisan agendas.

1. What is the PPRI Public Consultation programme?

The consultation programme invites citizens, policy professionals, researchers, and local leaders to contribute questions, observations, and evidence regarding Pakistan's provincial architecture, local-government devolution, and administrative governance. It provides a transparent channel for public input into policy analysis.

2. Does submitting an idea mean PPRI endorses or adopts it?

No. PPRI does not advocate for specific political parties, lobby groups, or boundary campaigns. Preparing or submitting an idea simply helps researchers identify the questions, priorities, and reservations that citizens care about most. Every analytical model is evaluated strictly against constitutional, fiscal, and administrative evidence standards.

3. What happens after an idea is submitted?

Submissions are cataloged and reviewed by PPRI researchers to identify recurring themes, empirical data references, and institutional trade-offs. Sourced ideas and unique research questions feed directly into PPRI's working paper series, scenario modeling, and research agenda updates.

4. Is PPRI proposing a specific new province?

No. PPRI examines multiple scenarios — from the existing 4-province baseline to administrative decentralization models (such as 12-unit or 16-unit scenario architectures) and empowered local government structures. Analytical scenarios are research tools designed to test institutional feasibility, not official government boundary proposals.

5. How does citizen consultation connect with the Scenario Lab?

The Scenario Lab allows users to test demographic distributions, administrative scales, and territorial models interactively. Citizen feedback helps define which indicators (e.g. population per district, hospital access distances, judicial workloads) matter most to regional communities.

6. Are new provinces the only reform option under study?

No. In fact, PPRI's core research demonstrates that provincial reorganization and constitutional local-government empowerment (Article 140A) must be studied together. In many instances, fiscal and administrative decentralization to elected local governments addresses citizen access needs without requiring territorial reorganization.

7. How can I submit a reform idea or question?

You can use the structured submission tool on the Submit an Idea page to draft your research proposal, or review the full questionnaire on the Survey page.
