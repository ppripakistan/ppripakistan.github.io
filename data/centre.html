Pakistan Public Data Architecture & Sub-National Indicators: Executive Data Summary

Executive Overview: Pakistan's governance and public administration operate across 241.49 million citizens (PBS Census 2023), distributed over four constitutional provinces, one federal capital territory, and two autonomous regions. As of FY2025–26, national macroeconomic and public finance data reflect structural centralizations: the 7th National Finance Commission (NFC) Award distributes 57.5% of the federal divisible pool to provinces, yet intra-provincial allocations remain concentrated in provincial capitals (Lahore, Karachi, and Peshawar), with limited devolved funds reaching Pakistan's 164 districts under Article 140A.

Empirical data across the 8 observatory domains reveals significant sub-national variance. Punjab encompasses 127.69 million people (52.88% of the federation), creating single-secretariat governance ratios of over 3.11 million citizens per district. In contrast, Balochistan covers 347,190 square kilometers (43.6% of national land area) with only 14.89 million people, producing extreme geographic distance to public services. Urban Sindh is anchored by Karachi Division's 20.38 million residents across 7 districts and 25 municipal towns, generating over 68% of provincial tax revenue, while rural districts exhibit severe human development gaps under PBS HIES 2024–25 surveys.

Under the 7th NFC Award, the federal divisible pool is distributed among provinces using four weighted criteria: Population (82%), Poverty/Backwardness (10.3%), Revenue Collection/Generation (5.0%), and Inverse Population Density (2.7%). Spatial Subsidiarity

Institutional & Judicial Access Constraints

Read Policy Brief

Citizens residing in peripheral districts face substantial temporal and financial friction to access provincial secretariats, departmental headquarters, and High Court principal seats. This spatial divide is a central driver of subnational discontent.

Gwadar & Kech (Makran) → Quetta

13.5 Hours

910 km over rugged regional highways. Requires multiple days of overland transit for routine constitutional writ petitions or administrative appeals.

Rajanpur & Rahim Yar Khan → Lahore

8.5 Hours

610 km from southern Punjab periphery to Civil Secretariat Lahore, reinforcing demands for south Punjab administrative decentralization.

Tharparkar (Mithi) → Karachi

6.5 Hours

410 km across desert terrain to reach provincial departments and specialized healthcare clusters in the provincial capital.

Chitral & Upper Dir → Peshawar

7.5 Hours

360 km through mountain passes with frequent weather disruptions, highlighting the necessity of Malakand regional governance autonomy.
