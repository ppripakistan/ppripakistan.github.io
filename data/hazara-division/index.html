1. What are the eight districts of Hazara Division?

Hazara Division is one of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa’s seven administrative divisions and encompasses eight districts extending from the Potohar-bordering industrial belt of Haripur up through the Indus Gorge into Upper Kohistan. In the 2023 Population and Housing Census (PBS KP Table 1), these eight districts recorded a combined population of 6,188,736, compared with a 2017 comparison figure of 5,323,155.

Where earlier editorial summaries cited legacy estimates (such as 5.7M, 5.86M, or 6.10M prior to full 8-district reconciliation), 6,188,736 is the verified Census 2023 eight-district total.

2. What the population tells us about public services

Dividing Hazara’s 6.19 million people into southern and northern tiers exposes the core administrative challenge behind local-government and provincial-budget debates. Mansehra, Abbottabad, and Haripur alone hold 4,391,032 residents (71.0%)—concentrated along comparatively accessible valleys, hospitals, colleges, and industrial estates linked to Islamabad and Peshawar. Meanwhile, the remaining 1,797,704 residents (29.0%) live across steep alpine slopes in Battagram, the three Kohistan districts, and Torghar, where road distances, winter closures, and scattered villages sharply raise the unit cost of operating schools, basic health units, and municipal water systems.

3. How does the division relate to the Hazara province proposal?

Analysts and students should strictly separate the existing administrative division from proposed province models:

  • Existing division (current law): Under the Constitution and Khyber Pakhtunkhwa’s administrative structure, Hazara Division is an eight-district division (6,188,736 residents in 2023) within Khyber Pakhtunkhwa.
  • Proposed Hazara province options: Historical legislative proposals—including Khyber Pakhtunkhwa Assembly resolutions in 2014 and 2020 and private constitutional bills—have generally envisioned elevating Hazara Division into a separate province, whereas subnational structural studies (including PPRI’s 13-unit scenario) examine separating the five southern/central Hazara districts (~5.15M) from an upper Indus Kohistan zone (~1.04M).
  • Constitutional threshold: Any alteration to Khyber Pakhtunkhwa’s territory requires two-thirds of the total membership of both Houses of Parliament plus a two-thirds resolution of the Khyber Pakhtunkhwa Provincial Assembly under Article 239(4).
