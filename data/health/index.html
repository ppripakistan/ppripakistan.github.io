Pakistan Public Health, Infant Mortality & Medical Infrastructure: Executive Summary

Executive Overview: Pakistan's healthcare landscape continues to face structural capacity constraints, characterized by a high infant mortality rate of 54.2 deaths per 1,000 live births and a maternal mortality ratio of 186 per 100,000 live births (Pakistan Demographic and Health Survey). Public health expenditure accounts for approximately 1.1% of GDP, resulting in severe resource ratios: 1 registered doctor per 980 citizens and 0.6 hospital beds per 1,000 people.

Stunting and malnutrition remain persistent public health emergencies: over 39.8% of children under 5 years suffer from moderate-to-severe stunting, with rates exceeding 46.6% in rural Sindh and 47.9% in Balochistan. Healthcare infrastructure is heavily concentrated in provincial tier-1 tertiary facilities, compelling millions of rural and secondary city residents to undertake emergency travel of 4 to 12 hours to access basic specialized care in provincial capitals.
