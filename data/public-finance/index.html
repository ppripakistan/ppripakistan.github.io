Pakistan Public Finance, NFC Divisible Pool & Fiscal Federalism: Executive Summary

Executive Overview: Public finance in Pakistan is anchored by the 7th National Finance Commission (NFC) Award signed in 2009 and enacted in 2010. Under Article 160(3A) of the Constitution, the provincial share of the federal divisible pool cannot be reduced below the baseline established in the preceding award. In FY2025–26, the gross federal divisible pool taxes administered by the Federal Board of Revenue (FBR) are projected at PKR 12.4 trillion, of which 57.5% (PKR 7.13 trillion) is transferred directly to the four provinces.

The multi-criteria horizontal distribution formula allocates funds among provinces based on four factors: Population (82.0%), Poverty and Backwardness (10.3%), Revenue Collection/Generation (5.0%), and Inverse Population Density (2.7%). As a result, Punjab receives 51.74% of the provincial pool, Sindh receives 24.55%, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa receives 14.62% (plus an additional 1% earmarked for war on terror costs), and Balochistan receives 9.09%. However, lack of statutory Provincial Finance Commission (PFC) devolution means over 85% of transfers remain centralized in provincial capitals, with minimal discretionary fiscal authority reaching municipal corporations.
