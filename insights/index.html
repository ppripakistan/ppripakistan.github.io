Visual Policy Analytics & Infographics Lab

The PPRI Visual Policy Analytics & Infographics Lab translates complex macroeconomic indicators, intergovernmental fiscal transfers, provincial budget allocations, and national household microdata into interactive, publication-grade empirical visualizations. Designed for policymakers, researchers, constitutional scholars, and civil society, this repository presents verified evidence on Pakistan’s core structural dilemmas: sovereign debt servicing monopolies, corporate tax expenditure concessions, cross-provincial fiscal and security disparities across Punjab, Sindh, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, and Balochistan, and the persistent erosion of real household purchasing power.

Core Analytical Policy Domains Covered ▾

The PPRI Visual Policy Analytics Lab synthesizes empirical visual evidence across four core structural policy domains:

1. Public Finance, Sovereign Debt & Fiscal Federalism: Visualizing how sovereign debt markup servicing absorbs 51.8% of total federal budget outlays (PKR 9,775B) and 76% of net FBR tax collection, the 8-year trajectory of sovereign public debt stock escalation (surpassing PKR 83.3T), distribution of PKR 3.98T in federal corporate tax exemptions and concessions, and cross-provincial budgetary allocations under the 7th National Finance Commission (NFC) Award.

2. Economic Transitions, Labour Absorption & Real Wages: Documenting the persistent erosion of real household purchasing power (-42% real minimum wage loss against +205% cumulative inflation since FY10), smallholder agrarian landholding fragmentation (<5 to >50 acres), sectoral employment absorption shifts, and 35 years of macroeconomic boom-bust cycles across four distinct governance regimes (FY90–FY24).

3. Demographics, Spatial Governance & District Urbanization: Charting provincial demographic growth trajectories across the 1981, 1998, 2017, and 2023 censuses, a 135-district urbanization versus population density quadrant scale, female agrarian landownership disparity, and 70 years of intercensal acceleration across Pakistan's 241.5 million population.

4. Living Standards, Household Food Security & Utilities: Examining regressive food Engel ratios where lowest-quintile households dedicate 52.4% of total consumption to food (HIES 2024–25), provincial food versus non-food consumption baskets, basic housing amenities and clean piped water deficits (48.2% national deficit), and Labour Force Survey gender wage penalties.

INFOGRAPHIC 04 / 16 Provincial Fiscal & Security Disparities • 4 Provinces Multi-Dimensional Comparison

Cross-provincial comparison of total budgetary outlay, Annual Development Programmes (ADP), outstanding public debt, per capita allocations, and regional terrorism burden

Key Policy Takeaway: Severe structural disparities exist between fiscal concentration and conflict exposure: Punjab and Sindh command 75.9% of all provincial budget spending, while Khyber Pakhtunkhwa (63.1%) and Balochistan (29.1%) bear 92.2% of Pakistan's terrorism fatalities. Furthermore, while Balochistan receives the highest nominal budget per person (PKR 64,158), its infrastructure allocation per km² (PKR 0.92M/km²) is less than one-seventh of Sindh's (PKR 6.81M/km²), reflecting severe territorial underdevelopment.
National Total Outlay: PKR 11.21 Trillion Total Prov Development: PKR 2.54 Trillion KP + Balochistan Conflict Share: 92.2% Fatalities Highest Debt Stock: Punjab (PKR 1.685T)
Source: Provincial Finance Departments (White Papers FY25), DMU Debt Bulletins, CRSS Annual Security Reports (2023–24) • PPRI

Peak Inflation: 30.8% (2023) COVID Contraction: -0.94% (2020) Peak Growth: 7.7% (2005) Current Debt/GDP: 74.8%

Multi-Census Provincial Demographic Shifts (1981–2023)

Decadal shifts in provincial demographic shares across 1981, 1998, 2017, and 2023 censuses

Punjab Shift: 56.1% → 52.9% Balochistan: 5.1% → 6.2% KP + FATA: 13.1% → 16.9%

Seven census milestones and intercensal annual growth rate trajectory

1951 Population: 33.7M 2023 Population: 241.5M Intercensal Growth: 2.55% p.a.
