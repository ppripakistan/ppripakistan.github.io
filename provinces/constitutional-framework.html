Constitution

The legal architecture matters.

Any serious discussion of additional provinces has to begin with the Constitution rather than with slogans or maps.

The Constitution defines Pakistan as a federal republic and identifies Balochistan, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, Punjab and Sindh among its provinces.

Article 1 — Federation and territories

Article 1 describes Pakistan as a Federal Republic and lists Balochistan, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, Punjab and Sindh among its provinces.

Article 140A — Local government

Article 140A requires each province, by law, to establish a local-government system and devolve political, administrative and financial responsibility and authority to elected local-government representatives. The Constitution also provides that local-government elections are held by the Election Commission of Pakistan.

Article 160 — National Finance Commission

Article 160 establishes the National Finance Commission framework for recommendations concerning distribution between the Federation and Provinces, grants-in-aid, borrowing powers and related fiscal matters. The constitutional text also protects the provincial share in each NFC Award from being lower than the share in the previous Award.

Article 239 — Constitutional amendment

Article 239 provides the procedure for constitutional amendment bills. A bill must receive the required two-thirds vote in each House. Where an amendment would alter the limits of a Province, it cannot be presented to the President for assent unless it has also been passed by the affected Provincial Assembly by not less than two-thirds of its total membership.

What this means for research

  • A new province is not simply an administrative notification.
  • A territorial change engages constitutional amendment procedures.
  • The affected provincial assembly's constitutional role is central where provincial limits would be altered.
  • Local-government reform is constitutionally distinct from creating a new province.
  • Fiscal design must account for the NFC framework and other intergovernmental arrangements.

Primary source: Constitution of Pakistan, Articles 1, 140A, 160 and 239.
