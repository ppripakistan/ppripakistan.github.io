Detailed Institutional Profiles

Deeper analysis of mechanics, risks, and implementation
Model A — Devolution First

Strengthened Local Governments

Argues that Pakistan's governance crisis is municipal, not provincial. Citizens require prompt birth certificates, functional primary schools, paved farm-to-market roads, clean drinking water, and responsive policing—none of which require redrawing provincial boundaries.

  • Key Instrument: Amendment to Article 140A requiring mandatory local elections within 90 days.
  • Primary Risk: Provincial legislators persistently starve local governments of funding and powers.
Best for: Rapid service improvements without border disputes
Model B — Territorial Restructuring

Creation of New Provinces

Focuses on the massive demographic imbalance of Punjab (53% of Pakistan) and geographical scale of Balochistan. Subdividing oversized units into 8–12 provinces breaks the single-province dominance in the National Assembly and shortens administrative distance to secretariats.

  • Key Instrument: Article 239(4) constitutional amendment passed by 2/3 majority in affected provincial assemblies.
  • Primary Risk: Replicating centralized provincial patronage in 8–12 new capitals without local devolution.
Best for: Correcting federal parliamentary scale imbalances
Model C — Combined Architecture

Provincial & Local Co-Design

Combines territorial reorganization with non-negotiable municipal devolution. Under this model, new provinces are created only on the condition that local governments are constitutionally protected from dissolution and guaranteed 45% of provincial revenue.

  • Key Instrument: Comprehensive Subnational Governance Constitutional Amendment Bill.
  • Primary Risk: Very high political bar requiring multi-party constitutional consensus.
Best for: Comprehensive long-term institutional stability
Model D — Administrative Decentralization

Regional & Metropolitan Authorities

Establishes statutory regional secretariats and metropolitan corporations without altering borders. Empowered Additional Chief Secretaries (ACS) and Additional IGPs are stationed in regional hubs (e.g. Multan, Bahawalpur, Sukkur) with delegated financial approval powers.

  • Key Instrument: Provincial rules of business revision and statutory authority enactments.
  • Primary Risk: Bureaucrats lack political accountability to regional electorates.
Best for: Immediate administrative relief without legislative deadlock

Detailed Profiles: 13 Proposed Administrative Units of Pakistan

Comprehensive empirical specifications for each subnational unit modeled under PPRI-01, detailing 2023 Census populations, division assignments, proposed capitals, and economic foundations.

27.5% of National Total

1. West Punjab (Central Industrial & Agrarian Heartland)

The most industrialized and densely populated proposed unit, comprising the provincial capital of Lahore, the textile powerhouse of Faisalabad, and the golden export triangle of Gujranwala, Sialkot, and Gujrat.

Divisions Included: Lahore, Gujranwala, Gujrat, Faisalabad, Sargodha (19 Districts)
Proposed Capital: Lahore
Census 2023 Population: 66.3 Million
Economic Foundation: Textile manufacturing, light engineering, IT services, financial headquarters, high-yield wheat/rice farming.
14.8% of National Total

2. South Punjab (Multan, DG Khan & Sahiwal Agrarian Belt)

Anchored by the historic regional metropolis of Multan, this unit consolidates the core Saraiki agrarian belt spanning the fertile lands between the Chenab, Ravi, and Indus rivers.

Divisions Included: Multan, Dera Ghazi Khan, Sahiwal (13 Districts)
Proposed Capital: Multan
Census 2023 Population: 35.7 Million
Economic Foundation: Cotton ginning, wheat cultivation, mango agro-processing, chemical fertilizers, regional trade logistics.
5.4% of National Total

3. Bahawalpur (Former Princely State Restoration Unit)

Reflects the constitutional restoration demand of the historic Bahawalpur State that acceded to Pakistan in 1947 and was merged into One Unit in 1955. Covers 45,588 km² stretching into the Cholistan desert.

Divisions Included: Bahawalpur Division (Bahawalpur, Bahawalnagar, Rahim Yar Khan - 3 Districts)
Proposed Capital: Bahawalpur
Census 2023 Population: 13.0 Million
Economic Foundation: Sugar refining, cotton spinning, canal-irrigated agriculture, historic heritage tourism, Islamic university education.
4.7% of National Total

4. Potohar (Northern Plateau & Twin-City Hinterland)

Encompasses the Potohar plateau surrounding the federal capital, historically characterized by high literacy, significant defense recruitment, and distinct rain-fed (barani) agro-ecological systems.

Divisions Included: Rawalpindi Division (Rawalpindi, Attock, Chakwal, Jhelum, Murree - 5 Districts)
Proposed Capital: Rawalpindi
Census 2023 Population: 11.4 Million
Economic Foundation: Defense administration, Attock oil refinery, cement manufacturing, foreign remittances, hill resort tourism.
8.4% of National Total

5. Urban Sindh / Karachi (National Maritime & Financial Megacity)

Pakistan's primary economic engine, housing the Karachi Port Trust (KPT), Port Qasim, State Bank of Pakistan, and Pakistan Stock Exchange (PSX). Addresses the unique municipal governance scale of the megacity.

Divisions Included: Karachi Division (7 Districts: Central, East, South, West, Korangi, Malir, Keamari · 25 Towns)
Proposed Capital: Karachi
Census 2023 Population: 20.4 Million
Economic Foundation: Port handling, maritime logistics, national banking/finance, automotive manufacturing, media, commercial exports.
9.2% of National Total

6. Rural Sindh / Lower Sindh (Indus Basin & Coastal Energy Corridor)

Stretches from Hyderabad across the Indus delta and the Thar Desert. Contains massive national energy reserves including the Thar Coal field and Jhimpir wind energy corridor.

Divisions Included: Hyderabad, Mirpur Khas, Shaheed Benazirabad (15 Districts)
Proposed Capital: Hyderabad
Census 2023 Population: 22.3 Million
Economic Foundation: Thar coal power generation, wind/solar energy, sugar production, sugarcane/rice cultivation, artisanal handicrafts.
5.1% of National Total

7. Mehran / Upper Sindh (Barrage Canal Agro-Industrial Zone)

Centered on the historic Sukkur Barrage on the Indus River. Serves as the critical tri-provincial trade gateway connecting Sindh, Balochistan, and Punjab.

Divisions Included: Sukkur, Larkana (8 Districts)
Proposed Capital: Sukkur
Census 2023 Population: 12.4 Million
Economic Foundation: Rice milling, date palm packaging, thermal power, natural gas exploration (Qadirpur, Kandhkot), inter-provincial logistics.
9.8% of National Total

8. Khyber (Peshawar Valley & Malakand Northern Zone)

The demographic and historical core of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, uniting the fertile Peshawar and Mardan valleys with the tourist, hydel, and mineral assets of Swat, Buner, Dir, and Chitral.

Divisions Included: Peshawar, Mardan, Malakand (16 Districts)
Proposed Capital: Peshawar
Census 2023 Population: 23.6 Million
Economic Foundation: Tobacco cultivation, hydel power generation, marble/granite mining, Torkham transit trade, national tourism.
4.6% of National Total

9. South KPK (Southern Pashtun Belt & Merged Tribal Districts)

Integrates the southern divisions of KP with newly merged tribal districts (North/South Waziristan, Kurram, Orakzai). Traversed by the CPEC Western Route (Hakla-DI Khan Motorway).

Divisions Included: Kohat, Bannu, Dera Ismail Khan (13 Districts)
Proposed Capital: Dera Ismail Khan
Census 2023 Population: 11.2 Million
Economic Foundation: Oil and gas reserves (Karak), gypsum, dry-port trade with Afghanistan, CPEC logistics, livestock pastoralism.
2.1% of National Total

10. Hazara (Hindko Cultural Zone & Karakoram Corridor)

Covers the predominantly Hindko-speaking Hazara region east of the Indus River. Connects the national capital region to Northern Pakistan and China via the Karakoram Highway (KKH).

Divisions Included: Hazara Division minus Kohistan (Abbottabad, Haripur, Mansehra, Battagram, Torghar - 5 Districts)
Proposed Capital: Abbottabad
Census 2023 Population: 5.1 Million
Economic Foundation: Hattar industrial estate, Kaghan/Naran eco-tourism, tertiary higher education, tea/horticulture, transit logistics.
0.4% of National Total

11. Kohistan (Upper Indus Hydel Energy Basin)

A specialized mountain alpine unit centered on the gorge of the Indus River. Critical to Pakistan's energy security as the site of Dasu Hydropower, Diamer-Bhasha Dam perimeter, and massive timber reserves.

Divisions Included: Lower Kohistan, Upper Kohistan, Kolai-Palas Districts (3 Districts)
Proposed Capital: Dasu
Census 2023 Population: 1.0 Million
Economic Foundation: Mega-hydropower royalties, forestry, mineral chromite, watershed ecology, adventure tourism.
5.4% of National Total

12. Balochistan Plateau (Central, Northern & Eastern Mineral Basin)

The central mineral and highland territory of Balochistan, spanning vast distances from Chagai's Reko Diq copper-gold deposits to the agricultural canals of Nasirabad and the fruit valleys of Quetta and Ziarat.

Divisions Included: Quetta, Kalat, Nasirabad, Zhob, Sibi, Loralai, Rakhshan (34 Districts)
Proposed Capital: Quetta
Census 2023 Population: 13.0 Million
Economic Foundation: Copper-gold-chromite mining (Reko Diq, Saindak), Sui natural gas fields, apple/grape/almond orchards, border trade with Iran & Afghanistan.
0.8% of National Total

13. Makran Coastal Unit (Southwestern Maritime Gateway)

Spans Pakistan's southwestern coastline along the Gulf of Oman, anchored by the CPEC deep-sea port city of Gwadar and the regional educational/administrative center of Turbat.

Divisions Included: Makran Division (Gwadar, Kech/Turbat, Panjgur - 3 Districts)
Proposed Capital: Turbat / Gwadar
Census 2023 Population: 1.85 Million
Economic Foundation: Deep-sea port operations, Free Economic Zone logistics, marine fisheries, date palm agriculture, maritime cross-border trade.
