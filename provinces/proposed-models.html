Detailed Institutional Profiles

Deeper analysis of mechanics, risks, and implementation
Model A — Devolution First

Strengthened Local Governments

Argues that Pakistan's governance crisis is municipal, not provincial. Citizens require prompt birth certificates, functional primary schools, paved farm-to-market roads, clean drinking water, and responsive policing—none of which require redrawing provincial boundaries.

  • Key Instrument: Amendment to Article 140A requiring mandatory local elections within 90 days.
  • Primary Risk: Provincial legislators persistently starve local governments of funding and powers.
Best for: Rapid service improvements without border disputes
Model B — Territorial Restructuring

Creation of New Provinces

Focuses on the massive demographic imbalance of Punjab (53% of Pakistan) and geographical scale of Balochistan. Subdividing oversized units into 8–12 provinces breaks the single-province dominance in the National Assembly and shortens administrative distance to secretariats.

  • Key Instrument: Article 239(4) constitutional amendment passed by 2/3 majority in affected provincial assemblies.
  • Primary Risk: Replicating centralized provincial patronage in 8–12 new capitals without local devolution.
Best for: Correcting federal parliamentary scale imbalances
Model C — Combined Architecture

Provincial & Local Co-Design

Combines territorial reorganization with non-negotiable municipal devolution. Under this model, new provinces are created only on the condition that local governments are constitutionally protected from dissolution and guaranteed 45% of provincial revenue.

  • Key Instrument: Comprehensive Subnational Governance Constitutional Amendment Bill.
  • Primary Risk: Very high political bar requiring multi-party constitutional consensus.
Best for: Comprehensive long-term institutional stability
Model D — Administrative Decentralization

Regional & Metropolitan Authorities

Establishes statutory regional secretariats and metropolitan corporations without altering borders. Empowered Additional Chief Secretaries (ACS) and Additional IGPs are stationed in regional hubs (e.g. Multan, Bahawalpur, Sukkur) with delegated financial approval powers.

  • Key Instrument: Provincial rules of business revision and statutory authority enactments.
  • Primary Risk: Bureaucrats lack political accountability to regional electorates.
Best for: Immediate administrative relief without legislative deadlock
