Implementation Architecture & Sequencing

The 7-Stage Subnational Transition Framework

Constitutional reform cannot be executed overnight by legislative proclamation. PPRI provides an operational roadmap sequencing legal enactment, boundary demarcation, personnel apportionment, NFC fiscal reallocation, and service continuity safeguards.

The 7-Stage Operational Transition Roadmap

Chronological execution sequence for institutional reform
Stage 01

Legal Settlement

Passage of Article 239(4) supermajority amendments and transition legislation.

Stage 02

Territorial Demarcation

Cadastral surveys, district polygon freezing, and administrative border gazetting.

Stage 03

Assets & Personnel

Civil service cadre apportionment, pension trust funds, and immovable asset inventories.

Stage 04

Fiscal Settlement

Interim NFC revenue sharing formulas and provincial revenue authority establishment.

Stage 05

Service Continuity

Ring-fencing hospitals, schools, policing commands, and judicial registries.

Stage 06

Institutional Launch

Constituency delimitation, provincial assembly elections, and secretariat inauguration.

Stage 07

Post-Reform Audit

Auditor General reviews, citizen satisfaction surveys, and 3-year governance evaluations.

Operational Protocols & Checklists

Technical deliverables required for each transition phase
Stage 01 — Months 1 to 6

1. Constitutional & Legal Settlement

Enacting the primary legal architecture. Requires an amendment to Article 1(2) (territories of Pakistan), Article 59 (Senate allocation), and passage by two-thirds of the total membership of the affected Provincial Assembly under Article 239(4).

  • Deliverable: Parliamentary Subnational Transition Act defining interim authority.
  • Key Risk: Legislative challenge before the Supreme Court under the basic structure doctrine.
Milestone: Presidential assent and gazette notification
Stage 02 — Months 7 to 12

2. Territorial Demarcation & Asset Cadastre

The Survey of Pakistan, in coordination with provincial Board of Revenue authorities, freezes district boundaries and prepares official cadastral maps. Solves contested border tehsils and allocates state-owned agricultural lands and water canal headworks.

  • Deliverable: Statutory boundary gazetteer signed by Boundary Delimitation Commission.
  • Key Risk: Inter-district disputes over water irrigation channels and barrages.
Milestone: Complete digitized boundary polygons
Stage 03 — Months 13 to 20

3. Personnel Apportionment & Pension Funds

Civil servants in the Pakistan Administrative Service (PAS) and Police Service of Pakistan (PSP) are allocated between parent and successor provinces. Provincial civil servants are granted domiciled options with seniority protections. Unfunded pension liabilities are divided based on actuarial audits.

  • Deliverable: Establishment of successor Civil Service Cadres and dedicated Pension Trust Funds.
  • Key Risk: Senior civil servants refusing relocation from historical metropolitan capitals.
Milestone: 100% staff allocation notifications issued
Stage 04 — Months 21 to 28

4. Fiscal Federalism & NFC Settlement

President convenes an interim National Finance Commission (NFC) meeting to determine horizontal distribution shares for the newly recognized federating units. Successor provincial revenue boards (e.g. Sales Tax on Services) are established with digitized billing infrastructure.

  • Deliverable: Presidential Order notifying revised NFC horizontal transfer percentages.
  • Key Risk: Severe liquidity shortfall during the initial fiscal quarter of autonomous budgeting.
Milestone: Independent State Bank provincial accounts opened
Stage 05 — Ongoing

5. Public Service Continuity Safeguards

Zero-interruption protocols: District Headquarters Hospitals (DHQs), rural health centers, high schools, police stations, and criminal courts remain fully operational. Mutual recognition agreements ensure academic certificates and medical licenses retain automatic legal validity across boundaries.

  • Deliverable: Inter-Provincial Service Continuity Memorandum signed by Chief Secretaries.
  • Key Risk: Temporary supply-chain disruptions in centralized pharmaceutical procurement.
Milestone: Zero operational service disruptions verified
Stage 06 — Months 29 to 36

6. Institutional Launch & Democratic Elections

Election Commission of Pakistan (ECP) completes constituency delimitations for the newly constituted Provincial Assembly. General elections are conducted, the Governor and Chief Minister are sworn in, and the new High Court Principal Seat begins hearing appellate matters.

  • Deliverable: First inaugural session of the successor Provincial Assembly.
  • Key Risk: Protracted coalition negotiations delaying first provincial budget passage.
Milestone: First autonomous provincial budget passed
Stage 07 — Post-Reform Audit

Independent Governance Performance Evaluation

Thirty-six months post-inauguration, the Auditor General of Pakistan and independent policy research bodies conduct an empirical evaluation: Did administrative travel times decrease? Have literacy and immunization rates improved? Are recurrent establishment overheads sustainable?
