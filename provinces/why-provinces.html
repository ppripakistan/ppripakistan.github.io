Reform Rationale & Inquiry

Why Do People Argue for New Provinces?

The debate over creating new subnational units in Pakistan is often driven by political rhetoric. PPRI dismantles the debate into five testable empirical hypotheses, examining demographic scale imbalances, administrative travel distance, legislative voice, and the local devolution counter-hypothesis.

650+ km Peak Travel Distance to Provincial Capital

The Five Core Arguments Under Scrutiny

Separating political advocacy from testable institutional claims
Argument 01

Administrative Scale & Distance

When a single provincial government administers over 127 million residents, executive decision-making becomes highly centralized in the provincial secretariat. Residents in peripheral districts face immense friction, multi-day travel, and bureaucratic delays to access appellate courts, departmental secretaries, and specialized public services.

Empirical Metric: Average citizen transit time to secretariat
Argument 02

Legislative Voice & Senate Parity

In the National Assembly, seats are apportioned strictly by population, giving Punjab an absolute majority of provincial seats (141 of 266 general seats). In the Senate, each province receives 23 seats regardless of size. Advocates argue new provinces would rebalance parliamentary dynamics and provide under-represented regions with constitutional veto power.

Empirical Metric: Provincial voting weight in joint parliamentary sessions
Argument 03

Targeted Development & Public Finance

Provincial Annual Development Programmes (ADPs) frequently face allegations of metropolitan bias, with capital infrastructure concentrated in mega-cities like Lahore or Karachi. A smaller territorial unit could theoretically prioritize regional industrial corridors, canal water distribution, and district human capital investments tailored to local geography.

Empirical Metric: Per capita ADP expenditure by division
Argument 04

Frontline Service Delivery Ratios

Provincial line departments manage education, health, policing, and municipal revenue. In oversized provinces, the span of control for an Inspector General of Police (IGP) or Chief Secretary spans dozens of millions of citizens, leading to weak oversight, delayed disciplinary reviews, and sluggish administrative response to local emergencies.

Empirical Metric: Civil servant to population supervisory span
Argument 05

The Devolution Counter-Hypothesis

Would creating new provincial secretariats simply replicate centralized, unresponsive power at a slightly smaller scale? PPRI tests whether meaningful financial, political, and administrative devolution to elected local governments under Article 140A can achieve better service access without the multi-billion rupee transition costs of new provinces.

Empirical Metric: Article 140A municipal fiscal share

Hypothesis vs. Empirical Evidence Matrix

Testing reform claims against institutional data
Reform Hypothesis Proponent Rationale Institutional Counter-Evidence Alternative Devolution Path
Administrative Proximity Shorter travel distance to provincial capital and departmental heads. High establishment costs for new secretariats, courts, and staff quarters without guaranteed front-line improvement. Empowered Deputy Commissioners and Divisional Commissioners with devolved spending ceilings.
Budget Allocation Parity Direct entitlement to provincial NFC transfers and independent budget control. Small provinces may lack own-source tax bases (e.g. GST on services), remaining heavily dependent on federal transfers. Statutory Provincial Finance Commission (PFC) awards guaranteeing 40%+ of provincial revenue to districts.
Regional Identity & Voice Dedicated constitutional representation in the Senate, CCI, and NEC. Risks ethnic gerrymandering and border disputes over mixed districts and water canal headworks. Regional Senate committees and guaranteed minority representation in provincial assemblies.
Public Service Efficiency Smaller spans of control allow tighter governance over schools, hospitals, and police. Fragmenting civil service cadres can dilute technical capacity, judicial recruitment, and senior expertise. Full implementation of Article 140A with directly elected mayors managing municipal services.
