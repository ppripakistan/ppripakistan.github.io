Research question

Why do people argue for new provinces?

The case for additional provinces contains several distinct claims. Each can be tested separately.

1. Representation

Advocates may argue that a very large province can make provincial institutions feel distant or concentrate political attention in particular areas. A smaller provincial unit could create another elected government and legislature. The empirical question is whether this would improve meaningful representation and accountability.

2. Administrative access

Provincial governments manage or coordinate major public services and institutions. Advocates may argue that a smaller territorial unit can shorten administrative chains and bring decision-making closer to residents.

3. Development priorities

Different regions can have different geographic and economic needs. A separate provincial government could theoretically tailor policies to a smaller territory, but this claim needs evidence about existing resource allocation and service outcomes.

4. Political voice

A new province can create new provincial-level political institutions. The relevant research question is how this changes representation relative to existing National Assembly, Senate, provincial and local-government institutions.

5. The counter-question

Some objectives associated with new provinces may also be pursued through stronger local government, administrative decentralization, metropolitan governance or changes to fiscal arrangements. PPRI therefore treats the reasons for reform as hypotheses to test rather than conclusions.
