Article 140A Constitution of Pakistan: Local Government Law, Devolution & Judicial Precedents

What Is Article 140A of the Constitution of Pakistan?

Article 140A of the Constitution of Pakistan establishes local government as a mandatory constitutional third tier. It requires each province to devolve political, administrative, and financial responsibility and authority to elected local representatives. Added under the Legal Framework Order 2002 and reinforced by the 18th Amendment in 2010 with Clause (2), it places the constitutional duty on the Election Commission of Pakistan (ECP) to hold local body elections.

1. Verbatim Constitutional Text: Article 140A

Article 140A: Local Government

"(1) Each Province shall, by law, establish a local government system and devolve political, administrative and financial responsibility and authority to the elected representatives of the local governments."

"(2) Elections to the local governments shall be held by the Election Commission of Pakistan."

2. The Tripartite Devolution Mandate

The constitutional wording of Article 140A(1) is conjunctive: it requires devolution across three distinct dimensions simultaneously:

  • Political Devolution: Direct election of local councils, mayors, nazims, and councilors, prohibiting bureaucratic administrators or unelected caretaker committees from superseding representative bodies.
  • Administrative Devolution: Placing line departments (elementary education, basic healthcare, water and sanitation, municipal waste, land-use zoning, building control) under the direct supervision of elected local councils rather than provincial secretariats.
  • Financial Devolution: Mandatory establishment of statutory Provincial Finance Commissions (PFCs) with predictable, formula-based revenue sharing from provincial divisible pools to local tiers.

4. Landmark Supreme Court Jurisprudence

The Supreme Court of Pakistan has repeatedly intervened to enforce Article 140A against provincial executive encroachment:

MQM-P v. Province of Sindh (PLD 2022 SC 343)

Authoritative verdict delivered by Chief Justice Gulzar Ahmed. The Court held that provincial governments cannot enact laws that strip local governments of their core administrative powers, ordering the restructuring of Sindh's local government statute to restore municipal authority over building control, water, and master planning.

Local Government Elections Delay Case (2020)

The Supreme Court ruled that provincial governments are constitutionally barred from dissolving local governments before the expiry of their constitutional terms and cannot run municipal governance indefinitely via appointed bureaucrats.

5. The Provincial Finance Commission (PFC) Bottleneck

While the National Finance Commission (NFC) Award guarantees predictable formula-based transfers from the federal divisible pool to provinces (under Article 160), provincial governments have consistently failed to establish institutionalized PFC awards for districts. As a result, less than 12% of total provincial development expenditures are currently determined or executed at the elected municipal level.
