Administrative Scale & Representation in Pakistan

An empirical framework for evaluating whether territorial size, citizen proximity, and administrative span of control justify subnational restructuring in Pakistan.

By PPRI Governance & Scale Research Desk Published: October 2026 Citation: PPRI-PB-001 Focus: Territorial Governance & Scale
60.37M Avg Population Per Province (Global Outlier)
164 Total Administrative Districts Across 4 Provinces
3.55M Average District Population in Punjab
14+ Hrs Max Travel Time from Peripheral Districts to Capital

1. Executive Summary & The Scale Paradox

Pakistan presents one of the most extreme structural paradoxes in contemporary comparative federalism. With 241.5 million people governed across just four constitutional provinces, the average subnational unit contains over 60 million citizens. Punjab alone, with 127.7 million residents, would constitute the 11th most populous country on earth if independent.

When a single provincial Chief Secretary and Cabinet preside over a population larger than the United Kingdom, France, or Germany, traditional administrative span of control inevitably breaks down. Institutional capacity becomes congested in the primate capital, paper processing creates immense bureaucratic friction, and peripheral districts experience government as a distant, abstract entity. Yet, international comparative evidence proves that smaller territorial units are not an automatic panacea. Without municipal autonomy and fiscal sustainability, subdividing provinces merely replicates centralization at a smaller scale.

The Core Scale Proposition

"Territorial scale matters, but not in isolation. A unit of 15 million people with weak municipal councils will deliver worse public services than a unit of 50 million people with empowered local governments and decentralized circuit benches."

3. The PPRI Five Diagnostic Tests of Administrative Scale

Rather than relying on vague political slogans, PPRI establishes five empirical criteria to evaluate any proposal for territorial reorganization:

Test 1: Physical & Institutional Accessibility

Can 90% of the unit’s population reach principal executive offices, High Court benches, and tertiary medical facilities within a 3.5-hour one-way journey via public transit?

Pakistan Status Quo: Fails severely in Balochistan (Gwadar to Quetta is 14+ hours) and South Punjab (Rajanpur to Lahore is 9.5 hours).

Test 2: Executive Span of Control & Bureaucratic Congestion

Does the administrative apex (Chief Secretary, Inspector General, Health Secretary) supervise more than 15–20 district-level operational units?

Pakistan Status Quo: Punjab has 41 districts and 127M citizens under a single secretariat, exceeding optimal organizational span by over 200%.

Test 3: Fiscal Viability & Own-Source Revenue (OSR) Base

Can the prospective territorial unit generate at least 15–25% of its recurring non-salary expenditures through municipal and provincial taxes (GST on services, urban immovable property tax, motor vehicle levy)?

Pakistan Status Quo: Highly uneven. Sindh (SRB) and Punjab (PRA) maintain moderate revenue bases, while Balochistan (BRA) relies on federal divisible pool transfers for over 92% of revenue.

Test 4: Judicial Architecture & Dispute Resolution Radius

Does the constitutional framework guarantee high-tier appellate justice without forcing litigants into multi-day stays in provincial capitals?

Pakistan Status Quo: High Court principal seats are isolated; circuit benches (Multan, Bahawalpur, Rawalpindi, Sukkur, Abbottabad) provide vital relief but possess constrained roasters.

Test 5: Sub-Provincial Devolution Immunity

Does creating a new territorial boundary safeguard elected municipal councils under Article 140A, or does it simply establish a smaller provincial oligarchy that strangles district nazims?

PPRI Warning: Creating smaller provinces without constitutional local government protection will merely multiply provincial bureaucracies without empowering grassroots citizens.

4. Key Policy Recommendations for the National Agenda

1. De-link Restructuring from Ethnic Partisanship

Frame provincial reorganization around administrative scale, geographic proximity, and travel time isochrones rather than linguistic boundaries to secure cross-party consensus.

2. Enact Article 140A Enforcement Legislation

Amend Article 140A to constitutionally prohibit provincial governments from dissolving elected local bodies before their full 4-year tenure expires.

3. Polycentric Institutional Dispersion

Disperse subnational headquarters across secondary cities (e.g. Faisalabad, Multan, Sukkur, Hyderabad) rather than concentrating all ministries in a single primate capital.
