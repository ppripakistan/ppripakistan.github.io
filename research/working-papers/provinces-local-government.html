Provinces & Local Government

A comparative framework for territorial restructuring versus constitutional local-government devolution in Pakistan.

By PPRI Intergovernmental Relations Desk Published: September 2026 Updated: October 2026 Citation: PPRI-WP-001
Art. 140A Mandates 3-Fold Devolution: Political, Admin, Fiscal
100+ UCs Per District Average, Creating Extreme Municipal Granularity

1. The Devolution Paradox: Territorial Restructuring vs Municipal Autonomy

In Pakistani policy debate, citizen grievances over distant capitals, poor municipal service delivery, potholed rural roads, and absent primary healthcare are frequently framed as arguments for creating new provinces. However, public finance and constitutional law identify a fundamental analytical disconnect: are these problems generated by oversized provinces, or by castrated local governments?

When a municipal grievance—such as sewage cleanup in Multan, a water pipeline in Sukkur, or primary school teacher absenteeism in Turbat—requires approval from an entrenched provincial secretariat hundreds of kilometers away, citizens understandably demand provincial re-carving. Yet establishing a new provincial apparatus without devolving municipal powers merely moves the bottleneck from Lahore or Karachi to a regional city, leaving the neighborhood citizen just as disempowered.

The Core Institutional Distinction

Provincial Reorganization (Art. 239) alters the horizontal federal units, Senate seats, and high-level legislative powers. Local Devolution (Art. 140A) alters vertical subsidiarity, placing revenue, sanitation, land zoning, and policing oversight directly in the hands of elected ward and town representatives.

2. Article 140A vs Article 239: Comparative Constitutional Pathways

The 1973 Constitution provides two fundamentally distinct institutional levers for addressing peripheral alienation. The following matrix contrasts their legal mechanisms, political requirements, and governance impacts:

Comparative Parameter Territorial Restructuring (Art. 239) Local Government Devolution (Art. 140A)
Constitutional Threshold Two-thirds majority in National Assembly & Senate, plus two-thirds of affected Provincial Assembly (Art. 239(4)). Mandatory statutory requirement under Art. 140A; enactable via simple majority provincial act.
Fiscal Mechanism Re-negotiation of National Finance Commission (NFC) Award under Art. 160; new divisible pool shares. Provincial Finance Commission (PFC) awards distributing provincial consolidated fund to districts.
Administrative Focus Creates new Governor, Chief Minister, Provincial Assembly, Secretariat, and High Court branch. Empowers Mayor, District Councils, Union Councils, and local municipal service corporations.
Service Delivery Realm Tertiary healthcare, policy regulation, provincial highways, university charters, macroeconomic planning. Water supply, solid waste management, primary clinics, elementary schools, local road networks, street lighting.
Transition Friction High: Asset distribution, civil cadre partitioning, capital infrastructure, debt liability apportionment. Moderate: Financial devolution to existing district administrations and functional transfer to elected councils.

3. Pakistan's Three Competing Local Government Blueprints

Local government in Pakistan has suffered from chronic legislative instability. Three competing institutional blueprints have alternated over the past quarter-century:

Model 1: 2001–2009

NRB Devolution of Power (LGO 2001)

Abolished the colonial Executive Magistracy and District Management Deputy Commissioner. Placed police and district departments under elected Nazims.

  • Elected District Nazim headed district governance
  • PFC rules institutionalized rule-based formula
  • Overturned by provincial assemblies post-2008
Model 2: 2013–2018

Provincial Acts of 2013 (Recentralization)

Restored bureaucratic primacy through Deputy Commissioners. Severely constrained municipal taxation and retained major subjects in provincial secretariats.

  • Indirect mayoral elections and limited fiscal powers
  • Creation of parallel provincial companies (e.g. Saaf Pani, LWMC)
  • Erosion of PFC transfers; discretionary funding
Model 3: 2019–2024

Hybrid & Neighborhood Councils (PLGA 2019/2021)

Direct mayoral election experiments in Punjab and KP, coupled with village and neighborhood councils. Frequent legal challenges delayed implementation.

  • Direct popular mandate for city mayors
  • Neighborhood councils with independent development grants
  • Dissolved multiple times by political transitions

4. Governance Problem Diagnostic: Which Tool Solves Which Problem?

To evaluate whether an administrative complaint demands territorial re-carving or municipal devolution, PPRI applies a 5-dimension functional diagnostic:

Governance Grievance Effective Lever: New Province Effective Lever: Local Govt Analytical Rationale
Urban Sanitation & Solid Waste Disposal ✗ Ineffective ✓ Optimal Provincial secretariats cannot monitor street-by-street garbage pickup; requires hyper-local accountability.
Tertiary Healthcare & Specialized Hospitals ✓ Optimal ✗ Sub-optimal Teaching hospitals require provincial capital budgets, civil service medical cadres, and high capital investment.
Equal Voice in National Senate ✓ Optimal ✗ Inapplicable Senate representation is strictly provincial (equal 23 seats per province under Art. 59); municipalities have zero representation.
Zoning Regulations & Building Encroachments ✗ Ineffective ✓ Optimal Centrally dictated master plans fail; requires town planners answerable to locally elected councilors.
High Court Circuit Bench & Judicial Access ✓ Optimal ✗ Inapplicable Constitutional writ petitions under Art. 199 require High Court jurisdiction, which follows provincial or bench lines.

5. The Constitutional Settlement: A Dual-Track Devolution Agenda

PPRI's working paper concludes that pitting provincial reorganization against local government is a false dichotomy. A functional federal model requires a dual-track settlement:

Core Policy Recommendations

  1. Constitutional Shielding of Local Governments: Amend Article 140A to guarantee fixed 4-year tenures for local councils, prohibiting arbitrary provincial dissolution and mandating continuous Election Commission supervision.
  2. Mandatory PFC Intercepts: Tie federal NFC releases to the active operationalization of Provincial Finance Commissions, ensuring at least 35% of provincial divisible pool funds flow directly to district and municipal accounts.
  3. Regional Administrative Divestment: Prior to constitutional boundary carving, establish fully autonomous Divisional Secretariats headed by Additional Chief Secretaries with delegated financial powers up to Rs 500 million.
  4. Subnational Fiscal Cadres: Establish specialized municipal service cadres for urban management, property tax assessment, and environmental enforcement, insulating local administration from politicized provincial transfers.
