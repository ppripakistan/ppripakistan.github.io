Academic Faculty & Fellows

Researchers & Contributors

PPRI convenes scholars, constitutional jurists, fiscal economists, and spatial analysts dedicated to evidence-based public policy on federalism and institutional design.

Multi-Disciplinary Research Faculty

Our working groups unite specialist expertise across four core institutional domains: Constitutional Jurisprudence, Fiscal Federalism (NFC & PFC), Local-Government Devolution, and Spatial Demography (GIS).

Working Group Chairs & Research Leads

Constitutional Jurisprudence

Working Group on Constitutional Architecture

Focuses on Articles 1, 140A, 160, and 239 of the Constitution of Pakistan. Examines legislative processes for provincial reorganization, judicial jurisdictions, and intergovernmental council mechanisms (CCI).

Key Publication: The Provincial Reorganization of Pakistan
Public Finance & NFC

Working Group on Fiscal Federalism

Analyzes horizontal and vertical fiscal equalization, provincial tax bases, transition overhead costs, and formulas for Provincial Finance Commission (PFC) awards.

Key Publication: What Would Reform Cost? A Fiscal Framework
Administrative Scale & Devolution

Working Group on Local Government & Deconcentration

Investigates the empowerment of District Councils, civil service transition hierarchies, divisional administrative secretariats, and police operational independence.

Key Publication: Provinces & Local Government
GIS & Quantitative Analytics

Working Group on Spatial Demography & Data

Responsible for the PPRI Data Observatory, 2023 Digital Census cross-tabulations, district accessibility radii, and the interactive Scenario Lab.

Interactive Tool: Scenario Lab GIS Engine

Visiting Fellows & Guest Contributors

PPRI regularly hosts visiting scholars, independent economists, and former civil administrators who contribute empirical working papers, legal commentaries, and data reviews.

Policy Contributions

Peer Review & Guest Research Standards

All contributions published under PPRI series are subjected to double-blind technical review for methodological rigor, empirical accuracy, and non-partisan neutrality. Contributors retain academic independence, and their analyses reflect empirical findings rather than institutional advocacy.

Call for Research Fellows & Academic Collaborators

Are you an economist, constitutional scholar, or urban planner researching Pakistani federalism, public finance, or local governance? PPRI welcomes proposals for policy briefs, working papers, and collaborative datasets.
