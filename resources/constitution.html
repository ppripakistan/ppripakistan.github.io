Statutory & Constitutional Reader

Constitutional Architecture of Provincial Reform

An annotated, clause-by-clause legal explorer examining the four constitutional pillars governing territorial boundaries, local devolution, fiscal distribution, and amendment thresholds.

Article 239(4): Altering Limits Article 1: Territories Article 140A: Local Govt Article 160: NFC Award Article 172: Natural Resources
The Federal Constitutional Gatekeeper

Article 239(4): Constitution Amendment Bill

“A Bill to amend the Constitution which would have the effect of altering the limits of a Province shall not be presented to the President for assent unless it has been passed by the Provincial Assembly of that Province by the votes of not less than two-thirds of its total membership.”

1. The Provincial Veto Power

Unlike in India (where Parliament can alter state boundaries under Article 3 after merely consulting state legislatures), Pakistan's Article 239(4) grants the affected province an absolute constitutional veto. A 2/3 federal majority in the National Assembly and Senate cannot alter a province's boundaries unilaterally.

2. Meaning of "Total Membership"

The threshold specifies two-thirds of the total membership of the provincial assembly, not two-thirds of members present and voting. For example, in the 371-member Punjab Assembly, at least 248 affirmative votes are required.

3. The Multi-Province Sequence

If a reform scenario involves splitting multiple provinces (e.g. establishing both a South Punjab province and an administrative division in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa), each respective provincial assembly must independently pass the amendment by a 2/3 supermajority.

Territorial Definition

Article 1: The Republic and its Territories

“(1) Pakistan shall be a Federal Republic to be known as the Islamic Republic of Pakistan.
(2) The territories of Pakistan shall comprise:
    (a) the Provinces of Balochistan, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, Punjab and Sindh;
    (b) the Islamabad Capital Territory, hereinafter referred to as the Federal Capital;
    (c) such States and territories as are or may be included in Pakistan, whether by accession or otherwise.”

Textual Enumeration

Because Article 1(2)(a) explicitly lists the four provinces by name, creating any new province fundamentally requires a formal Constitutional Amendment Act modifying this specific subsection.

Senate Representation Adjustment

Article 1 interacts directly with Article 59 (The Senate). Under Pakistan's bicameral design, each province is guaranteed equal representation (currently 23 senators per province). Creating new provinces requires amending Article 59 to define whether new units receive equal or modified Senate representation.

Local Government Devolution

Article 140A: Local Government

“(1) Each Province shall, by law, establish a local government system and devolve political, administrative and financial responsibility and authority to the elected representatives of the local governments.
(2) Elections to the local governments shall be held by the Election Commission of Pakistan.”

The Tripartite Devolution Mandate

Article 140A does not merely mandate advisory committees; it constitutionally requires the devolution of political, administrative, and financial authority. Supreme Court jurisprudence has repeatedly confirmed that provincial governments cannot arbitrarily dissolve elected local councils.

The Devolution Alternative

In PPRI’s research framework, robust implementation of Article 140A serves as a direct policy alternative or complement to provincial creation. Empowering District Councils provides citizens with local governance without necessitating federal boundary amendments.

Fiscal Equalization & NFC

Article 160: National Finance Commission

“(3A) The share of the Provinces, in each Award of National Finance Commission shall not be less than the share given to the Provinces in the previous Award.
(4) As soon as may be after the recommendation of the National Finance Commission has been received, the President shall, by Order, specify the share of the net proceeds of the taxes which is to be allocated to each Province...”

The 3A Ratchet Clause

Inserted by the 18th Constitutional Amendment, Article 160(3A) prevents the federal government from reducing the provincial share below 57.5% of the divisible pool, ensuring long-term fiscal predictability.

Commission Reconstitution

The NFC is composed of the Federal Minister for Finance and the Finance Ministers of the Provinces. If the federation expands to 12 or 16 provinces, the NFC would expand accordingly, requiring refined consensus mechanisms for award approvals.

Natural Resources & 18th Amendment Co-Ownership

Article 172: Ownerless Property, Minerals, Oil & Natural Gas

“(1) Any property which has no rightful owner shall, if located in a Province, vest in the Government of that Province, and in every other case, in the Federal Government.

(2) All lands, minerals and other things of value within the continental shelf or underlying the ocean within the territorial waters of Pakistan shall vest in the Federal Government.

(3) Subject to the existing commitments and obligations, mineral oil and natural gas within the Province or the territorial waters adjacent thereto shall vest jointly and equally in that Province and the Federal Government.”

1. The 50-50 Joint Ownership Rule

Inserted by Section 65 of the 18th Constitutional Amendment (2010), Article 172(3) replaced exclusive federal ownership with a landmark 50-50 co-ownership model between the federal federation and the producing province for on-shore and adjacent offshore hydrocarbons.

2. Natural Gas Royalties & Wellhead Levies

Article 172 operates in synergy with Article 161 (Natural Gas and Hydro-electric Power). Under Article 161(1), net proceeds of federal excise duties and royalties on natural gas are transferred straight to the province where the wellhead is situated (e.g. Sui gas fields in Balochistan, Badin and Ghotki in Sindh).

3. Fiscal Implications for Sub-Units

When proposing territorial reorganization (such as carving out a South Punjab province, a Makran coastal unit, or a Hazara unit), revenue distribution from mineral deposits (Reko Diq, Saindak copper-gold) and gas fields must be codified. Article 172 ensures mineral wealth directly benefits the contiguous provincial treasury.
