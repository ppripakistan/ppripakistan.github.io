Scenario 1 Status Quo Reference

The Four-Province Baseline: Scale, Concentration & Inertia

Pakistan's existing administrative configuration dates back to the dissolution of One Unit in 1970 and its reaffirmation under the 1973 Constitution. Across the 53-year span between 1972 and 2023, Pakistan's population surged from 65.3 million to 241.49 million (a 270% expansion), yet the federation continues to govern through just four constitutional provinces plus the federal capital territory.

This institutional freeze has created unprecedented governance ratios globally. Punjab alone houses 127.69 million citizens—making it more populous than Japan, the United Kingdom, France, or Germany, yet governed by a single Chief Minister, a single Chief Secretary, and a single centralized Civil Secretariat located in Lahore.

Primary Institutional Bottlenecks

  • The Capital City Magnet: Over 45% of provincial Annual Development Programme (ADP) funds historically concentrate in capital districts (Lahore, Karachi, Peshawar), leaving peripheral divisions (DG Khan, Thar, Kohistan, Makran) critically deprived of basic infrastructure.
  • Administrative Distance: Citizens in Rahim Yar Khan or Rajanpur must travel over 550 km (9 to 11 hours) to access appellate secretariats or High Court principals in Lahore; citizens in Gwadar face 750 km to Quetta.
  • Bureaucratic Chokepoints: Critical statutory files (teacher postings, hospital equipment sanctions, irrigation permits) bottleneck at centralized department desks in provincial headquarters.

Fiscal Distortions of the 7th NFC

  • Population Predominance: The 7th National Finance Commission (NFC) Award assigns 82% weighting to population, heavily rewarding large demographic aggregations and disincentivizing provincial sub-division.
  • Intra-Provincial PFC Collapse: While provinces successfully demanded fiscal devolution from Islamabad under the 18th Amendment, all four provincial governments dismantled Provincial Finance Commission (PFC) mechanisms, withholding statutory fiscal transfers from district tiers.
  • Subnational Fiscal Dependency: Provinces collect less than 8% of national tax revenue themselves, relying almost entirely on federal transfers from the FBR Divisible Pool.
Scenario 2 Territorial Reorganization

Territorial Restructuring: Sub-Dividing into Viable Administrative Units

The territorial restructuring pathway evaluates redrawing Pakistan's internal provincial boundaries into smaller, more homogeneous administrative units. Under PPRI's benchmark modeling (the PPRI-01 13-Unit Framework), existing administrative divisions are coalesced into autonomous subnational provinces with populations ranging between 12 million and 25 million citizens.

Proposed Unit

South Punjab / Saraiki

Core Divisions: Multan, Bahawalpur, D.G. Khan (11 districts).

2023 Population: 39.52M (30.9% of Punjab).

Proposed Capital: Multan / Bahawalpur (dual secretariat).

Addresses historic regional neglect, irrigation water equity, and civil service posting proximity.

Proposed Unit

Hazara Province

Core Divisions: Hazara Division (8 districts: Abbottabad, Haripur, Mansehra, Battagram, Kohistan).

2023 Population: 6.13M (15.0% of KP).

Proposed Capital: Abbottabad.

Resolves 18th Amendment naming dispute, linguistic distinctness (Hindko), and Himalayan tourism/hydel revenue rights.

Proposed Unit

Bahawalpur Restoration

Core Region: Former Princely State (Bahawalpur, Bahawalnagar, Rahim Yar Khan).

2023 Population: 13.01M.

Proposed Capital: Bahawalpur.

Based on the 1955 Accession Agreement, pre-One Unit provincial status, and agricultural revenue self-sufficiency.

Constitutional & Financial Feasibility Analysis

The Article 239(4) Hurdle: Article 239(4) of the Constitution mandates that no bill altering the limits of a province can be presented to the President unless passed by a two-thirds supermajority of the provincial assembly of the affected province. This gives dominant provincial political majorities in Lahore and Karachi an absolute constitutional veto over territorial partition.

Institutional CAPEX & Startup Costs

PPRI econometric modeling estimates the startup capital expenditure for each new province at PKR 28.5 to 36.2 Billion ($100M–$130M USD) over a 3-year gestation period. This covers:

  • Civil Secretariat complex and ministerial chambers.
  • Provincial Assembly building and legislative infrastructure.
  • High Court Principal Seat and bar accommodation.
  • Provincial Public Service Commission (PPSC) and Police Headquarters.

The Senate Parity Conundrum

Pakistan's Senate provides equal representation to all federating units (23 senators per province). If Punjab is divided into 3 provinces while Sindh and Balochistan remain intact:

  • The newly carved provinces would together control 69 Senate seats, disrupting the delicate federal balance established in 1973.
  • Any constitutional amendment must either recalibrate Senate quotas (e.g. proportional or weighted) or maintain regional balance across all federating units.
Scenario 3 Devolution Alternative

Empowered Local Government: Municipal Devolution Under Article 140A

An alternative pathway that achieves decentralized governance without redrawing provincial boundaries or amending Article 239 is the comprehensive operationalization of Article 140A of the Constitution. Inserted via the 17th Amendment and fortified by the 18th Amendment, Article 140A mandates:

"Each Province shall, by law, establish a local government system and devolve political, administrative and financial responsibility and authority to the elected representatives of the local governments."

Despite this constitutional imperative, Pakistan's provincial assemblies have repeatedly enacted oscillating local government laws (PLGA 2013, 2019, 2021; SLGA 2013, 2021) that strip municipal bodies of taxation authority, place elected mayors under the arbitrary control of non-elected Deputy Commissioners, and suspend councils for extended bureaucratic interregnums.

The 4 Pillars of PPRI Municipal Architecture

  1. Statutory PFC Guarantee: Amend the Constitution to require that provinces transfer a mandatory minimum of 45% of their provincial divisible pool directly to district and municipal governments on a formula matching NFC equity standards.
  2. Municipal Cadre Autonomy: Devolve primary healthcare (BHUs, THQ hospitals), primary and secondary education, municipal water, and district roads directly to elected District Chairs and Mayors.
  3. Protected Constitutional Tenure: Constitutional amendment barring provincial governments from dissolving or suspending local councils prior to the completion of their 4-year term without Supreme Court concurrence.
  4. Local Property Tax Retention: 100% of Urban Immovable Property Tax (UIPT) and local betterment levies retained by municipal corporations rather than routed through provincial finance departments.

Empirical Benefits vs New Provinces

  • Near-Zero Capital Setup Cost: Utilizes existing 164 District Council buildings, Tehsil headquarters, and municipal corporation infrastructure. Zero new civil secretariats or High Courts needed.
  • Immediate Proximity: Decisions made within 15 to 30 km of ordinary citizens rather than moving the capital from Lahore to Multan (still 150 km from outlying citizens).
  • No Senate Disruption: Leaves the 4-province constitutional federation, Senate quotas, and national legislative balance entirely intact.
  • Political Feasibility: Requires provincial legislative compliance and Supreme Court enforcement under Article 140A rather than contentious two-thirds constitutional amendments.

Scenario 5 Global Comparative Governance

International Comparative Federalism: Lessons for Pakistan

Subnational boundary and administrative reform is not unique to Pakistan. Examining historical precedents from other major federal democracies provides invaluable empirical insights into how administrative restructuring impacts economic growth, ethnic stability, and governance delivery.

South Asian Precedent

India: 1956 States Reorganisation & New Small States

India faced identical post-colonial administrative dilemmas. Through the States Reorganisation Act of 1956 and subsequent constitutional amendments (creating Uttarakhand, Jharkhand, and Chhattisgarh in 2000, and Telangana in 2014), India grew from 14 states to 28 states and 8 union territories governing 1.4 billion people.

  • Economic Impact: Empirical econometric studies indicate that in the decade following statehood, Uttarakhand and Chhattisgarh recorded annual GSDP growth rates 2.4% higher than parent states (UP and MP), driven by decentralized infrastructure spending.
  • Key Lesson for Pakistan: Article 3 of the Indian Constitution grants Parliament unilateral authority to reorganize state borders with simple majority, avoiding the provincial veto trap of Pakistan's Article 239(4).
Post-Colonial Federalism

Nigeria: From 3 Mega-Regions to 36 States

At independence in 1960, Nigeria was divided into three massive ethnic regions (Northern, Western, Eastern). Regional polarization and centrifugal competition led directly to the Biafran Civil War (1967–1970). In response, Nigeria fragmented its internal borders through sequential military decrees into 12, then 19, 21, and ultimately 36 states.

  • Stability vs Fiscal Drag: While breaking mega-regions successfully neutralized ethnic secessionism, it resulted in catastrophic bureaucratic bloat. Over 85% of Nigerian state budgets are consumed by recurrent civil service salaries, leaving negligible capital for development.
  • Key Lesson for Pakistan: Creating new subnational units without indigenous tax collection capabilities creates chronic fiscal dependency on central divisible pools.
Cooperative European Federalism

Germany: Cooperative Federalism & Equalization

Germany's Basic Law distributes authority across 16 Bundesländer (ranging from North Rhine-Westphalia with 18M people to Bremen with 680,000). Rather than competitive federalism, Germany enforces cooperative federalism.

  • Länderfinanzausgleich: The constitutional fiscal equalization mechanism automatically redistributes tax revenues between financially strong states (Bavaria, Baden-Württemberg) and weaker states to guarantee living standards parity (*Einheitlichkeit der Lebensverhältnisse*).
  • Bundesrat Governance: State governments directly comprise the upper house, voting as unified delegations rather than individual party politicians.
Subsidiarity Standard

Switzerland: 26 Cantons & Absolute Subsidiarity

Switzerland's federation of 26 cantons and 2,132 municipalities represents the global pinnacle of the subsidiarity principle—the rule that central government should only perform tasks that cannot be performed at a more local level.

  • Fiscal Sovereignty: Swiss municipalities and cantons set their own direct income tax rates, collecting over 60% of all public revenue at the sub-federal tier.
  • Key Lesson for Pakistan: True decentralization requires taxing authority at the municipal level; without own-source revenue, local councils remain mere administrative begging bowls.
Scenario 6 Modern Public Infrastructure

Technology & Artificial Intelligence for Subnational Governance

A transformative dimension often missing from traditional Pakistani administrative debates is the role of digital public infrastructure (DPI) and artificial intelligence. PPRI research establishes that much of the geographic friction and administrative extortion documented in Pakistan is an artifact of manual, opaque paper-based bureaucracies. Leveraging modern technology can achieve decentralization outcomes without massive physical brick-and-mortar overhead.

1. Automated AI Grievance Triage

Replace passive portal complaint forms with conversational, multilingual (Urdu, Punjabi, Pashto, Sindhi, Balochi, Saraiki) AI models that instantly classify, verify, and route citizen complaints directly to the designated Tehsildar or District Officer.

Imposes automated statutory resolution countdowns; unaddressed complaints automatically escalate to Divisional Commissioners and the Provincial Ombudsman with public audit trails.

2. Algorithmic PFC Smart Ledgers

Eliminate discretionary political withholding by replacing manual finance department releases with algorithmic smart ledgers.

As soon as the federal FBR Divisible Pool transfer clears the State Bank of Pakistan, the Provincial Finance Commission formula automatically calculates and executes straight-through electronic transfers directly into municipal council treasury accounts, bypassing bureaucratic interference.

3. Satellite ADP Project Verification

End the epidemic of "ghost" infrastructure schemes across peripheral divisions.

PPRI proposes linking Annual Development Programme (ADP) contractor milestone payments to automated high-resolution satellite imagery verification and geotagged timestamped field imagery, ensuring public rupees are only disbursed for physically verified schools, clinics, and asphalt pavements.

Cadastral Digital Twins & Eliminating Patwari Monopolies

Over 70% of civil litigation in Pakistan's subordinate judiciary stems from rural land boundary disputes, fraudulent registry mutations, and discretionary rent-seeking by revenue officials (*Patwaris*). Deploying modern GIS drone cadastral mapping and immutable digital title registries eliminates the physical necessity for citizens to travel hundreds of kilometers to district land offices, dismantling a core driver of administrative distance in Pakistan.

PPRI Synthesis & Policy Recommendation

The Optimal Integrated Reform Pathway

PPRI's holistic empirical evaluation concludes that the single most viable, immediate, and cost-effective subnational reform strategy for Pakistan is a two-pronged phased architecture:

  1. Immediate Phase (Months 1–18): Full Article 140A Municipal Empowerment with a constitutional amendment guaranteeing statutory PFC revenue awards (45% minimum) and municipal tenure protection, powered by AI grievance triage and digital land administration.
  2. Medium-Term Phase (Years 2–5): Target Administrative Division Reorganization focusing specifically on the high-consensus, demographically critical units (South Punjab and Hazara) under a rationalized Senate parity compact that maintains national federation stability.
